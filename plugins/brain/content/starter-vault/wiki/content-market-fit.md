# Content Market Fit

Ideation rooted in market research is the greatest skill set a creator can have.

## Core Principles

### The Topic is the #1 Most Important Part of a Video

This framework is emphatic about this: the topic (the idea of the video) matters more than the hook, the editing, the storytelling, the pacing, or any tactical trick.

The most important part of a video is not the hook. Not the edit. Not the storytelling. Not the pacing. The number one most important part is the topic - the idea of the video.

People think hooks, scripting, and editing make a video go viral. It is the topic. People want to know topics. The best edit in the world on a vague or irrelevant topic gets ignored.

A concrete example: a wrestling coach went from zero to 10K followers on Instagram in 14 days. The reason was not fancy hooks or editing - it was knowing the deep topics wrestlers actually care about. Instead of generic videos like "ways to get more takedowns" (boring, obvious), the approach was recovery tips after hard weight cuts - a deep pain point every wrestler privately obsesses over but few creators address.

The first video: weight cut recovery, how to eat every single meal and still have a great weight cut. That blew up. Not "three ways to do a single leg" or "three ways to win more matches."

### Content Market Fit (Borrowed from Startups)

The best cross-research industry for thinking about content is startups. How they start up and how they scale is very similar to how a page grows on Instagram.

The startup parallel:

**Minimal Viable Content (MVC)** - Your most basic, easiest-to-produce format. Literally turning on the camera and talking. No fancy production, no editing, just the working system.

In a startup, you want to get your minimal viable product as fast as possible. It is a shoe with no design - just the sole and the fabric. You want to sell it to 100 people. If 100 people buy it, then people actually like this concept.

**Content Market Fit** - When you consistently get views, your audience responds, and you know what resonates. The threshold is roughly 3,000-5,000 views per video consistently, maybe up to 10-15K.

If you are spewing off 100, 200, 300, maybe 500 to 1000 views, you have not found content market fit. So why would you invest in hyper-produced content if you have not found it yet?

**Then Scale** - Only after content market fit do you increase volume or invest in production. Y Combinator says nothing else in the world matters except finding product-market fit - the same applies to content.

Y Combinator: nothing else in the world matters, except for finding product market fit. Sprint there. All of your resources should go there. Do not do any funny business until you have found that fit.

The practical process: Sprint to 100 posts on your MVC. Your only goal is to improve your super-easy production method and find what resonates. Post with different angles until you find your content market fit. Once found, compress your efforts using the accordion method (see [[posting-frequency]]) into only your winners.

Important caveat: content market fit must happen within your brand pillars. A random viral video on an off-brand topic does not count as content market fit.

### The Iceberg of Niche Vocabulary

Every niche has surface-level topics (top of the iceberg) that everyone talks about, and deep topics (below the waterline) that only insiders and experts discuss. The deep topics are where the gold is.

In every niche, there are the classic top-of-iceberg words: seeds, planting, soil, tilling. Every gardener thinks about those regularly. But nothing is profound there. Elite gardeners are not talking about soil and basic things. They are probably talking about some metric related to sun ray intensity or hydroponics ratios.

The test: Imagine 20 experts in your niche sitting in a circle, like an AA meeting. What are they talking about? They are NOT talking about the basics. They are talking about the deep vocabulary - the concepts, frustrations, and insights that only come from real experience.

Application: Low-level content creators make videos about "how to go viral" - that is top of the iceberg. But in a room with 20 experienced creators, they would be talking about: how to monetize a large following, how to build community, brand pillars, being authentic while feeling pigeonholed, when to expand to other platforms.

People are so tired of the idea of virality as a topic. Videos about going viral do not do crazy well. Instead, based on market research and time in niche, the real problem creators have is having a big following and not being able to monetize it.

Once you find a deep niche vocabulary word (like "hydroponics" in gardening, or "weight cutting" in wrestling), move it directly into your on-screen text hook. That keyword is what stops the scroll for your target audience.

### TAM (Total Addressable Market)

TAM determines the ceiling of any video's view count. The Family Feud analogy: if you ask "what is the best food in the world?" and the top answer is pizza at 54%, pizza has the highest TAM. Clams might be your passion, but clams have a tiny TAM.

Whenever you make a video, it goes to a certain TAM. If you make it about pizza, that reaches the most TAM. If you make it about clams, that reaches a really small TAM.

The practical question: "Out of 100 people in my niche, how many deeply observe this?"

Real example: A client in the career/job hack space made a video about a "conference networking playbook." A virality consultant pointed out the TAM was tiny - not many people attend conferences. The fix: talk about work culture (everyone has observed it), job interviews (everyone has done one), getting a promotion (universal desire).

The consultant said: not very many people have observed that event or circumstance. Talk about work culture. Everyone has observed work culture. Everyone has observed a job interview.

Many creators think their videos are underperforming when they have actually maxed out their TAM. If you make a video for "ladies at Arizona State who are interested in Pilates," that is about 20,000 people. Getting 2,000 views on that video means you reached 10% of your TAM - that is actually phenomenal.

If you say "Hey, ladies at Arizona State who are interested in Pilates," that is only about a 20,000-person TAM. If that video gets more than 20,000 views, you reached not who you were trying to reach.

Use AI for TAM analysis - it is one of the legitimate uses of AI for content creation. Challenge yourself to go higher TAM while staying relevant to your niche.

### Going Viral is Often Bad

A counterintuitive warning: virality can actually hurt your account.

Going viral on social media is one of the worst things you can do for your account.

Why? 98% of the time, viral content is not connected to your main money-maker. You get a million views, attract followers who do not care about your niche, those followers do not engage with your next videos, the algorithm sees low engagement from followers and suppresses your reach.

Going viral and making money are complete opposites. 98% of the time a viral piece of content is not connected to your main money-maker, which means you get a bunch of views and you make no money.

A virality consultant confirmed: it is harder to build a loyal following over time when you focus on virality. The goal should be consistent high performance within your niche, not random spikes of irrelevant attention.

## Ideation as the #1 Skill

### Time Allocation

Spend 40% of all content creation time on ideation. Not hooks, not editing, not filming - ideation.

Spend at least 40% of your time scraping other competitor content, YouTube, your own data, Reddit, anything in the world to make sure your topic selection is strong.

Full time allocation:

- 40% ideation
- 20% growth tactics (trial reels, comment farming, automation tools)
- 15% strategy (top/middle/bottom funnel balance)
- 10% hooks
- 5% scripting/storytelling
- 5% editing/production
- 5% copywriting skill development

### Ideation is What Separates Stuck Creators

On the "view ladder," most creators plateau in the 800-1,700 view range because of poor ideation - not because of hooks or editing.

The full view ladder breakdown:

**0-300 views**: Answer a fundamental question - why should someone watch you? Lip-syncing in the mirror is not a good enough reason. Giving people three tips on how to get rich when you are not rich is not a good enough reason. Get 10 other people to answer this question about you honestly.

**300-1,000 views**: Still at the basics. Focus on understanding content fundamentals.

**800-1,700 views (the stuck zone)**: This is where most creators live and die. It is an ideation problem, not a hooks or editing problem. Find the specific subjects your audience actually wants to hear about.

**1,000-3,000 views**: Now hooks, pacing, and storytelling matter. You understand the basics and need to level up communication.

**10,000-50,000 views**: Data analysis and advanced communication. Break videos into chapters, figure out which chapter types win, use literary devices (metaphors, frameworks, equations).

**50,000+ views**: Content engine territory. Testing at scale, building a team.

### Where to Find Ideas

**1. Reddit** - Go to your niche's subreddit, sort by "top of the month" (not hot, not new, not controversial). Copy the entire conversation including replies into ChatGPT and ask for the two sides arguing, the sentiment, and the vocabulary they use.

Go to reddit.com. Sort by top conversation of the month. Copy and paste the whole entire transcript and open all of the replies. Copy and paste the whole entire conversation into ChatGPT and say: give me the two sides that are arguing. What is the sentiment between the two sides? Use the same words as them. Make your video positioned in the middle and then towards the end, take a side. This approach will immediately produce at least your first 5x outlier.

**2. Polls on Stories** - Do not ask "what do you want to hear about?" (lazy). Instead, run polls with deep layered vocabulary options that force people to reveal their real interests. Run a poll and ask with deep layered vocabulary choices.

**3. Niche TikTok Doom Scroll** - Create a second TikTok trained only on your niche. Scroll looking for three things: unique formats to bring over, niche vocabulary and new information, and comments that reveal what people want.

**4. Comments on Your Own Videos** - The most underrated source. Look for topics people engage with. A client's video game skin review kept getting comments about one specific item. Next day they made a video about that item - it went viral.

**5. YouTube Comments and FAQs** - Go to popular websites and YouTube channels in your niche. Read the FAQs. Read the comments on popular videos. This is free market research.

**6. ChatGPT/Claude** - But NOT for generating ideas from scratch. Feed it pockets of data (Reddit threads, comments, FAQs, your own analytics) and let it distill patterns and find the underlying themes.

You have to go give it pockets of data, go to Reddit, go to YouTube comments, FAQs of popular websites in your niche. You cannot just ask it to search the internet and get good results.

**7. Twitter/X (the Hormozi Method)** - Alex Hormozi's entire testing system runs through Twitter. He posts thoughts, his team analyzes data on top-performing hooks and topics, then they turn winners into short-form and long-form content.

**8. The FDR Method** - FDR succeeded because he knew exactly what pain the American people were experiencing at each moment. Apply this to your niche: What is the exact pain your audience feels RIGHT NOW? Not last month - right now.

### The Contextualization Framework

One of the most powerful ideation frameworks: the ability to consume ANYTHING in the world and connect it back to your niche.

One thing that creators are absolutely terrible at is being able to consume the world and then apply it to their niche - to contextualize.

Most creators either copy directly (which fails because different niches have different viewing psychology) or run out of ideas entirely.

The process:

1. **External input** - Listen to a podcast, read a book, see a Takis bag, watch a movie
2. **Extract the underlying principle** - What actually made this interesting or successful?
3. **Find the relationship** to your niche's underlying principles
4. **Make a video** applying that principle to your niche

You have to be able to do this with a Takis bag. Is there something on a Takis bag that you can make a video about and move it back to your niche?

Example: A gardener sees a Takis bag with Red 40 warnings. They make a video: "Your carrots are dying because of something I call the Red 40 Takis method - you're putting this into your soil and it might seem fine because it's just a similar color, but it's actually killing the root."

If you can do this, you can consume the world and always have something to make content about. Listen to a song - video. Listen to a podcast - video.

Important nuance: the cross-niche bridge requires understanding that different audiences have different psychology. Women tend to prefer aspirational and lifestyle content. Men respond more to status, education, and social approval. Going from a male-dominated niche to a female-dominated niche (or vice versa) has complexities that a direct copy will not capture.

### Information Content is NOT Dead

Despite claims that AI and LLMs are killing educational content, this framework strongly disagrees:

You have to understand that AI tools work best when you know what to search for. The great thing about algorithms is that they find information that you did not know you needed. Information content will never go away because you will always need information to find you.

However, two things have changed:

1. Do not make SEO-style content that someone could find from a basic ChatGPT search
2. Stop copying - if your information is not unique or uniquely articulated, you will get run over

This is your red alarm to quit copying. You will not make it if you are copying information. Start innovating.

## Testing and Iteration

### The 70/30 Rule

The foundational split: 70% proven content (topics and formats you know work), 30% wild card experiments.

Basically do a 70 to 30 split. 70% should be stuff that you feel pretty confident will hit. 30% should be wild card videos when you are getting outside of your box.

### Testing Fields

Every other marketing medium has A/B testing - email subject lines, paid ads, magazine surveys. Content creators inexplicably just post whatever they want. This makes no sense.

Literally every other marketing medium has testing, A/B testing. But for some reason, content creators are like "I'm gonna post whatever I want." Makes absolutely no sense.

The math of testing: If you get 1% better every day organically, you reach 10,000 average views in 7.5 months. If you A/B test and find a winner that is just 5% better, then continue improving 1% per day, you reach 10,000 average views in 39 days. That is over 6 months faster.

If you A/B tested and found your winner and it was just 5% better, instead of reaching 10,000 views in seven and a half months, you get there in 39 days.

### Methods for Testing

**Trial Reels** - The greatest function that has ever been given to creators. Yap into trial reels 10-20 times a day. Observe which has the highest watch time. Every day, whatever topic you talked about that did best, make an actual polished video about it.

Yap into your trial reels 10 to 20 times a day. Observe which one has the highest watch time. Then every day, whatever you were talking about, move it over and make an actual good video on it.

**TikTok Burner** - Make a video, create two versions with different hooks and on-screen text, post both simultaneously on a TikTok burner account. After two hours, check which has the better hook rate. Move the winner to your main page.

Make a video, do two hooks, do two on-screen texts, post them both at the same time on a TikTok burner. After about two hours, see which one has the best hook rate. Move it over to the main page.

**Story Polls** - Run a poll on your story. Ask which of four video concepts they want to see. About 100 people reply that they want to see bicep curls rather than abs. Then make a video about bicep curls, not abs just because you want to talk about abs.

**Twitter/X (Hormozi Method)** - Post raw thoughts on X. Analyze which get the most engagement. Turn winners into produced content.

Alex Hormozi only posts on Twitter. His team analyzes the data of top-performing hooks and topics.

**The B-Roll One-Liner Method** - Animate 20 photos, overlay 20 one-liners sourced from Reddit and comments, post all as trial reels. Look for outliers and analyze the underlying buzzwords, emotions, and pain points.

### Exploiting Anomalies

When a video overperforms - even slightly - you need to dissect it and replicate what worked. This is the biggest mistake even large creators make.

When you make a piece of content and it goes viral, you need to learn how to exploit that anomaly.

The 1977 paper company story: a company's best clients were in Chicago. They assumed it was because of a great salesperson. But when that salesperson retired and the replacement performed equally well, a new president investigated and found the real cause: same-day delivery to Chicago. He implemented next-day delivery everywhere. Revenue went up across all locations. The lesson: find the real variable driving success, not the obvious one.

Three ways to exploit a content anomaly:

**1. The Hook** - Were there numbers in it? Were there keywords? How many words was the hook? A sweet spot for on-screen text hooks is usually around six words. Were there parentheses? Was there a unique angle?

**2. The Pacing** - Break the video into sections. Figure out what each section was (a famous person reference, a personal story, an action step, a joke, a quote). Which section drove the retention?

Break the video into timed sections: a 5-second hook, 20-second section, 20-second section, 20-second section. Figure out what was in each section that made it pop.

**3. The Ideation Layers** - Topics have layers. Carrots is a topic. Under carrots: watering techniques, soil management, trimming leaves. Under trimming leaves: what scissors to use, what utensils. A video about utensils pops? Now make a video about your top 10 favorite gardening utensils.

There are layers to this. After trimming the leaves, it could be what scissors you need to use. What utensils. Oh, that video popped. Now go talk about a different utensil.

### Data-Driven Decision Making (Card Deck Method)

The full system for making decisions based on data rather than intuition:

1. Take your three most recent videos with different view counts
2. Break each into "chapters" (ingredients)
3. Remove the flop entirely
4. Find commonalities between winners
5. Go into comments to see what people specifically engaged with
6. Isolate the winning chapter type and the winning topic
7. Combine winning chapters from different videos into one new video

In front of you: three different videos, and then the ingredients of the video - what could be called video chapters.

Result: a 6K-viewed video becomes a 77K video. That is how you use data.

### A Realistic Creator Journey (Case Study)

A real client journey shows what a realistic path looks like:

- **Week 1**: 3,000 followers from a previous fitness account. Trying to pivot to AI + Airbnb. Posted 20-30 pieces on the old account in the new niche - nothing worked. Made a new account.
- **Weeks 1-3**: New account, 300, 165, 114, 199 views. Stuck in 300-view jail. Trying talking head format, iterating every video - numbers on screen, keywords, pictures, different on-screen text. Nothing ripping.
- **Week 4**: Still the same. Trying backgrounds, glasses, object lessons. Probably 50-60 videos deep across both accounts.
- **Week 5-6**: Had a conversation about changing the format AND going higher TAM. Talking at a desk was not working. AI + Airbnb was too narrow.
- **Month 3 (Week 8)**: Going green screen, split screen, math problems on screen. Getting creative with formats.
- **Week 9**: Started getting closer to 1,000-view videos. A personal travel story with green screen hit 2,000 views. Pattern emerging: personal travel experiences.
- **Week 10+**: Added controversial takes. First 160K-view video (a hot take about the best place in Italy). Then 1.7 million views on "Japan in the United States" (a replicable format).

This is the creator journey for 99% of people. Most do not know how to iterate week over week - testing the glasses, the background, the on-screen text, what they are talking about, how to go up TAM and down TAM, how to change the format.

Total journey: approximately 80-100 videos across multiple accounts, 3+ months of iteration, before cracking the code.

## Finding Your Content Lane

### Niche Selection Through Monetization Lens

Your core brand pillar must be attached to something that makes other people money - if you want to build a business, not just a hobby.

Your first brand pillar needs to be attached to something that is going to make other people money: a tax strategist, email marketing, remote sales, helping people get jobs.

Example: Gardening is not a strong monetization niche by itself. But "gardening for locally sourced foods to restaurant farm-to-table" connects to helping restaurants make money.

Then add two personality pillars - things you personally enjoy that give people a different angle on your personality.

Example: Gardening (core) + Pokemon cards + Peloton (personality pillars). Someone who likes Peloton AND is a restaurant owner AND collects Pokemon cards sees you and thinks "this person is like me."

### Counter-Positioning (Full Breakdown)

Counter-positioning is how you break into a saturated niche. Three methods:

**1. Format** - Do what nobody in your niche does visually. Every fitness creator films in the gym? Do object lessons at a desk.

**2. Niche Pocket** - Go luxury, go beginner, or add a cross-niche flavor. Huberman added science to self-improvement.

**3. Branding/Messaging** - Position against the norm. If your niche is Miami gurus with Lamborghinis, be the person with a normal workspace in Omaha, Nebraska.

To find your counter-position systematically:

1. List the top 10 people in your niche
2. Use AI to describe what they talk about, their formats, and what they value
3. Write down your actual beliefs that are opposite to the majority
4. Do not fake it - if you are not goth, do not pretend to counter-position against fitness bros
5. Look at other niches for format and style inspiration to import

What do you believe that could be in juxtaposition with the other 10 people in your niche? Get it on paper and start actively exercising it.

### Amazon Expansion Model for Niches

Stage 1 (0-10K followers): **Crack into the market** - Be hyper-niche. Be specific. Counter-position. Accept that nobody cares about your build-in-public journey or day in the life yet.

Stage 2 (10K-250K): **Expand a little** - One main niche plus a few angles. Amazon went from books to CDs, music, and movies.

Stage 3 (250K+): **Talk about whatever you want** - Only after conquering.

What did Amazon do first? Books. It cracked into the delivery ecosystem with books, but Jeff Bezos knew the whole time he was going to deliver everything eventually.

You do not get the privilege to talk about whatever you want right now.

The selfishness trap: Creators want to talk about themselves, change niches, "become the niche." But where else in life do you succeed doing whatever you want?

If the quarterback of a football team was like "hey coach, I'm just gonna do whatever I want today." If Pizza Hut was like "on Tuesdays, we're not going to be doing pizza, I'm actually gonna turn this into a bookstore because I like books." Where else in your life can you be successful doing whatever you want?

Only about 1% of people have interesting enough personalities to "be the niche." A creator who is an absolute savage on the mic with a top-tier personality is in that 1%. Most people are not. Accept it and follow the brand pillar blueprint.

### North Star + Incremental Improvements

Two principles that 99.9% of creators lack:

**1. A clear North Star** - One explicit, specific goal. Not followers AND revenue AND a podcast AND brand deals AND a book AND a community. Your content cannot optimize for all of those simultaneously.

An estimated 99 out of a thousand creators could not explicitly explain what their north star is.

**2. Incremental improvements toward that North Star** - Using data to improve 1% per week toward the goal. Nobody does this because they are not even thinking intentionally in the first place.

If you do not have a plan to strategically attack your content, how are you going to improve on it? You cannot improve anything that you do not have qualified as a fixed variable in the first place.

Practical example: North star = follower growth toward 1 million. Figure out required weekly follower growth. Plan 7 videos per week with specific topics and formats. After the week, analyze: which 2-3 videos drove the most followers? What was their topic? Format? Hook rate? Mix the winning topic from one with the winning format from another. Repeat.

The amount of creators that do both of them is one out of ten thousand.

### Three Problems With Modern Content

A marketing veteran's view: modern content creators are like beginners in the grand scheme of marketing. Three specific problems:

**1. No incremental data improvements** - In every other marketing discipline (email, paid ads, website, TV trailers), teams analyze data and improve. Content creators just shoot from the hip.

**2. No psychology** - No research, no psychology books, no understanding of mass human behavior. Creators just make the type of content they want to make.

**3. No strategic connection** - No connection from one video to the next. In paid ads, there is a journey: ad to landing page to email to website to retarget ad. In content, each video exists in isolation.

Fix any one of the three and you are in the top 1% of content.

### The Feedback Loop is What Matters (Not Consistency)

The myth that posting consistency "trains the algorithm" is not accurate. Going 8 days without posting and the next video still performing at baseline proves this.

What consistency actually does: it speeds up your feedback loop.

All you are doing is speeding up your feedback loop. The feedback loop is the amount of time it takes you to deliver a product, get it into the hands of a customer, see if they liked it, then innovate and ship the second variation.

The baseball analogy: the team gathers data for 7 innings about a pitcher's tendencies. By the 7th inning, they know exactly what pitch is coming in a given count. Content works the same way - you post to gather data to innovate, not to train any platform system.

Three things to analyze after every video:

1. What was the idea/topic? (What subjects did I cover?)
2. How did I portray it? (What format? Talking head? Green screen? Vlog?)
3. What did the comments say? (What else were people engaging with?)

## Tactical Playbook

1. Spend 40% of your content time on ideation - not hooks, not editing, not filming
2. Find your niche vocabulary: what do 20 experts in a circle talk about? Go layers below the surface.
3. Use TAM analysis before every video - out of 100 people in your niche, how many observe this? Challenge yourself to go higher TAM.
4. Run the 70/30 split: 70% proven topics, 30% wild card experiments
5. Exploit anomalies: when something pops, break it into hook + pacing + topic layers and replicate each element
6. Test via trial reels (10-20/day), TikTok burner accounts, and story polls before committing to main page
7. Use the Reddit method: top of month, copy full conversation into ChatGPT, find two sides, use their vocabulary, position in the middle then take a side
8. Use the contextualization framework: external input - underlying principle - your niche connection - video
9. Track a north star metric and make incremental 1% improvements toward it weekly using the mix-and-match method
10. Start hyper-niche (Amazon with books), expand only after dominating your current lane
11. Counter-position against your niche's top 10: find format, niche pocket, or messaging differences
12. Pick brand pillars: one core pillar connected to making others money + two personality pillars
13. Stop making videos people could find with a basic AI search - innovate or die
14. Analyze feedback loops: post, analyze (topic/format/comments), innovate, repeat
15. Do not confuse a high-TAM viral video with content-market fit - it must be within your brand pillars

## Key Principles (Condensed)

- The most important part of the video is the topic, not the hook or editing.
- Ideation is the number one skill set of a great creator.
- Content market fit exists: the intersection of what you can make, enjoy making, and what the market wants.
- The iceberg of niche vocabulary: surface topics are overcrowded, deep vocabulary is where the gold is.
- TAM determines the ceiling of any video's view count. Many creators confuse maxed-out TAM for underperformance.
- Going viral is often bad for your account - it attracts the wrong followers.
- All content posting is to gather data for the feedback loop, not to train any platform system.
- Information content will never die because algorithms surface what people did not know they needed.
- Fix any one of three problems - no data, no psychology, no connection - and you are in the top 1%.
- A clear north star + weekly incremental improvements puts you in the top 0.01% of creators.
