# Counter-Positioning

Differentiation is survival. This principle, drawn from Jeff Bezos and applied to content strategy, sits at the core of everything in this framework.

Counter-positioning is the single most important skill for creators. It is "the number one skill in content" and "the number one skill, not just in content, but in marketing in general." It is the reason creators get stuck at 300 views, and the reason bigger creators cap at 250K followers and cannot break through. Videos on this topic routinely generate more engagement than any other subject in this space.

## Core Principles

### What Counter-Positioning Actually Is

Counter-positioning is finding what makes you different from everyone else in your niche and leaning into it hard. It is fundamentally about pattern interruption - standing out in a sea of sameness. But it goes deeper than just "being different." It is about understanding what the default looks, feels, and sounds like in your space - and then deliberately going the other direction.

In a world where billions of people and companies compete for attention, the very first step is sticking out. No other skill matters as much. As Seth Godin framed it: marketing is about arresting attention. If you don't interrupt the pattern, you don't even get a chance to persuade.

A vivid illustration: someone learned pattern interruption by using it to land a job with a mentor. The method was to create a unique video, get it into the mentor's inbox in a way he'd actually see it, and include a unique analysis of the mentor's business. Despite being underqualified, the pattern interruption made the mentor think: "this person actually understands how to break through the noise" - and it worked.

### The Pendulum Swing

Counter-positioning is not just about being different - it is about reading the pendulum of public sentiment and getting ahead of it.

If your content looks like Lambos, helicopters, and dollar amounts - there is a pendulum swinging. People are getting tired of the guru aesthetic that dominated 2020-2023, and they're wanting to swing back the opposite direction. Creators who counter-position against that fatigue are riding the pendulum.

This applies to every psychological medium. Fashion swings. Politics swings. Content swings. The question is always: where is the pendulum now, and where is it going?

### Uniqueness is the #1 Trait

Consider a quiz: which of five traits would you want as a creator - expert, unique, best communicator, wealthiest, or hottest? The only correct answer: unique.

The uncomfortable truth is that social media rewards uniqueness over expertise. A top Harvard research assistant who cannot communicate more uniquely than everyone else won't get followed. The platform is not merit-based in the traditional sense - it rewards distinctiveness.

What is rewarded on content is not who thinks best or who knows most. It is, more often than not, the person who communicates most uniquely.

The quiz breakdown:

- **Expert**: Being the #1 expert means nothing if you can't communicate uniquely. There are billion-dollar CEOs doing podcasts getting 50 views.
- **Unique** (correct answer): Dennis Rodman energy. You don't fit in, and that's the advantage.
- **Best communicator**: Decent short-term, but eventually people see through it. Better to be unique with a little communication than a great communicator who isn't unique.
- **Wealthiest**: The Rolex-and-chains approach is dying. People are tired of being told to want what someone else has.
- **Hottest**: Limited as a long-term strategy.

The model creator is one who is not extremely polished at communication, is reasonably expert in their field, but is genuinely unique in how they communicate and position themselves - and that uniqueness overrides both deficiencies.

### Why Copying Does Not Work

Copying people is no longer working because market cycles in content are moving so fast that people get burnt out. If you're making content that someone could have found from a basic ChatGPT search, you'll get run over.

Funnel cycles - what people find annoying and tune out - are compressing. You used to be able to run one funnel for a year. Now you might get two months. So copying someone's current approach means you're already executing a played-out strategy by the time you ship it.

### The Four Archetypes of Great Creators

Counter-positioning fits within a larger framework of what makes a great creator. The best online creators are a mix of four archetypes:

1. **10% Athlete**: Disciplined and competitive. Some creators go three years barely missing three posts a day.
2. **10% Teacher**: Not the most important - the best teacher does not always win online.
3. **20% Showman**: It's not who knows the most that wins on social media. It's whoever can communicate in a more unique way, a more compelling way, in a more memorable way - even if it's less valuable information.
4. **60% Marketer**: Great creators are at least 60% marketer. Marketers are experts in psychological influence - getting people to take action. The action in content is watch time. The viewer thinks they're choosing to watch - they're actually being psychologically guided by the creator's marketing skill.

Counter-positioning is the overlap of showmanship and marketing: be unique (showman) in a way that psychologically compels people (marketer).

## The Five Categories of Counter-Positioning

Five areas where creators can differentiate:

### 1. Brand Pillars

What do people think when they think about you? You should have three pillars. The worst approach: "my brand pillars are fitness, lifestyle, and entrepreneurship" - zero differentiation.

Instead, combine two things: fitness + paraplegic, entrepreneurship + third world country, content strategy + anti-guru. The best counter-positions come from overlapping two unexpected domains.

The anti-guru position is a clear example: modest house, cheap car, no expensive lifestyle - in a niche full of Miami gurus and expensive displays of wealth. Whether or not that person is actually more trustworthy, it reads that way.

**The Brand Pillar Framework:**

When picking brand pillars, pick three:

1. **Core pillar**: Must be attached to something that makes other people money. Tax strategy, email marketing, remote sales, helping people get jobs. Not just "gardening" but "gardening for farm-to-table restaurants."
2. **Personality pillar #1**: Something you genuinely like that gives a different angle. Pokemon cards, Peloton, vintage cars.
3. **Personality pillar #2**: Another personal interest that creates association.

Example: A gardener whose core pillar is "locally sourced foods for farm-to-table restaurants" with personality pillars of Pokemon cards and Peloton. Now restaurant owners who also like Pokemon cards or Peloton feel immediate kinship. The overlap creates community.

Examples of strong brand pillars:

- **Andrew Huberman**: Added science to self-improvement
- **George Heaton**: Lifestyle + clothing brand + high aesthetic

### 2. Format

The visual way your content comes across the screen. If everyone in your niche does talking head, do green screen. If everyone does green screen, do vlogs. If no one does object lessons, do those.

A concrete example: in the fitness niche, everyone does gym lifts and looks hot in the mirror. An object lesson at a table - nobody does that in fitness. The format alone creates separation.

A 10-minute talking head rant on TikTok - where nobody ranting for 10 minutes because the platform favors short content - is a format counter-position. It's essentially a YouTube video on a platform where nobody expects that length.

**Inventing Formats:**

Several formats have been invented and pushed into mainstream use by a single creator: the pen and paper desk format, the triple clone video ($1M founder, $10M founder, $100M founder), and AI carousels (split screen). The principle is that you no longer need to invent new knowledge. You just need to invent a new format.

Where to find format inspiration: television shows. Family Feud, ESPN, Modern Family, NCIS. Is there anything that can become an Instagram video rather than a talking head or a crazy edit?

**Format Tier List:**

- **S Tier**: Split screen, notes app green screen (with curiosity coverage), reaction with commentary, carousels
- **A Tier**: Object lessons, top down, ranking videos
- **B Tier**: Whiteboard, normal clone video
- **D Tier**: Day in the life, podcast repurpose, skits

**The Contextualization Framework:**

Most creators either directly copy formats from other niches (which fails because different niches have different viewing behaviors) or run out of ideas. The correct approach:

1. Observe an external input (podcast, someone else's video, a random object in daily life)
2. Identify the underlying principle that made it work
3. Find the relationship between that principle and your niche's principles
4. Turn it into content in your context

You have to be able to do this with anything. Is there something on a bag of chips that you can make a video about and move it back to your niche?

### 3. Delivery

How you communicate. The personality, pace, and style of your content.

Different delivery counter-positions in practice:

- Authentic, hilarious, energetic delivery
- Monotonous, analytical, nerdy delivery
- Unscripted, rambling, story-driven delivery
- Reading biographies and breaking them into episodes

Going deliberately unscripted in a space full of scripted content is itself a counter-position. The viewer gets rambling, pausing, weird tangents - and that natural quality stands out precisely because most content in that niche is polished.

Historical storytelling as delivery: If you always bring in statistics, research studies, or book breakdowns, that becomes a delivery counter-position.

**FDR as Delivery Master:**

Studying all 31 of FDR's fireside chats reveals a delivery principle: pain point awareness at scale. FDR could not have one-on-one conversations with Americans, so he had to understand the demographic and speak to their exact pain point. He described Americans as "intelligent but anxious" - an extremely rare emotional combination - and spoke calmly without oversimplifying because he knew his audience would see through anything less.

Application: Who is your audience? What are their pain points? Can you describe them in 2-3 emotions? "Intelligent but nervous. Young but playful. Work hard but exhausted. Overworked, underpaid."

### 4. Positioning (Niche Pocket)

Go upstream, downstream, or cross-stream in your niche.

- **Upstream**: Pool installations for luxury homes
- **Downstream**: Cheap, affordable pool installations
- **Cross-stream**: Pool installations in the Pacific Northwest, pool installations in hyper-humid climates

Real-world examples:

- **Shelby Sapp**: Female in high-ticket sales niche. Gender alone is a counter-position.
- **Andrew Huberman**: Self-improvement + science. Cross-niche counter-position.
- **Nike**: Athletics + killers (Kobe, Jordan) vs. Lululemon's softer association

### 5. Credibility

Ask yourself: "Why should someone listen to me?" Two things getting overplayed: making a lot of money, and having a title (PhD, MD). Instead, position through unique experience, unique story, unique take.

The framework: you shouldn't listen to me because of money, but because of the people I've worked with and because of how I think analytically about content. That's a credibility counter-position away from the typical income flexing.

Another example: positioning yourself as the CTO of a well-known company. Because of that role, people listen - not because you make a hundred thousand dollars a month doing coaching.

## How to Find Your Counter-Position

### The 10-Person Analysis Method

Step-by-step process:

1. **Take 10 people in your niche** - the top 10 that everyone is seeing
2. **Go into AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)** and describe: What they talk about mostly. The formats they use (talking head, green screen, etc.). What do they value (family, Miami guru life, time freedom)?
3. **Ask yourself**: What are your actual beliefs that are opposite? Don't make this stuff up. If you're not actually goth, don't go goth to counter-position. What do you genuinely believe that is in juxtaposition with the other 10 people in your niche?
4. **Get it on paper** and start actively exercising it
5. **Be inspired by other niches**: If you're a gardener, go look at the fitness niche, the data science niche. What formats are they using? What topics? What styles?

### The Three Tactics

1. **Add something to your niche**: Horse riding + AI. You stand out in both.
2. **Go upstream or downstream**: Horse riding for rich people, or horse riding for college students.
3. **Counter-position with association**: Huberman = self-improvement + science. Nike = athletics + killers (Kobe, Jordan). Who do you quote? Do you use math? Do you reference Steve Jobs?

### The Amazon Blueprint

You do not get to talk about whatever you want. Follow the Amazon expansion model:

1. **Crack into the market** (0-10K followers): Be hyper-niched, specific, counter-positioned. This is Amazon selling only books. You must dominate a tiny pocket first.
2. **Expand** (10K-250K): Add one or two layers. Amazon adding CDs, movies. You can start going slightly broader, adding a personality pillar or covering adjacent topics.
3. **Transcend** (250K+): Now you can talk about whatever you want. You earned it.

The path works like this: a creator starts in a specific space, then moves to business, then into more lifestyle, then transcends into general success - but does this through volume and maxing out each level before moving up. The key is not jumping immediately to broad topics while still early.

Critical warning: do not attempt transcendence until you have completely dominated your pocket. This strategy works for 1% of creators. The other 99% who try to transcend early lose their compound growth.

### The Niche Quality Quadrant

A useful quadrant for evaluating niches and counter-positions:

**Y-axis**: Connected to money (helps people make money) vs. Not connected to money
**X-axis**: High-ticket service (trade time for money) vs. Product/entertainment

- **Top right (best)**: Tax consulting, AI agencies, sales teams. With 10-15K followers, you can make $100K/month.
- **Bottom right**: Custom cabinets, plumbing, travel planning. High-ticket service but not directly money-connected.
- **Top left**: Fitness, productivity planners, therapy. Indirectly connected to money but can't charge high ticket.
- **Bottom left (hardest)**: Gaming, music, comedy, entertainment. Need massive following to monetize.

**The follower-to-dollar ratio:**

- Sales person with 500-600K followers: $4.40/follower ($2.2M in one day)
- Marketing person with 13K followers: $3.46/follower ($45K/month)
- Famous bodybuilder: $0.04/follower ($125K/month via brand deals)
- Influencer: $0.03/follower ($20K/month via brand deals)

How to move up and to the right: A comedian can bundle corporate shows. A musician can build a six-week curriculum and sell it to private schools. A cabinet maker can pivot to luxury office design consulting for CEOs.

### Using AI to Find Counter-Positions

The ROCO framework for AI-assisted counter-positioning:

1. **Role**: "You are a niche research specialist and you specialize in finding counter positions. You are phenomenal at understanding mass psychology."
2. **Objective**: "Build me a one-page counter position to my niche so I can stand out amongst the competitors."
3. **Context**: Pile in competitors, what they do, best-performing videos, niche details, passions, story. Use large context windows.
4. **Output**: Specify exactly what you want - five chapters, bullet points, three-sentence structures. Tell it exactly how you want your order received.

Critical: send each section separately and confirm understanding before proceeding. "Do you understand? Don't start yet."

### The Passion Trap

Most positioning problems are caused by the passion trap: choosing a niche you want to make content about, but not one that financially rewards you.

The fix: turn a skill into a passion instead of turning a passion into a skill. Your life will probably be better if you choose a high-value skill and fall in love with it over time.

Steve Jobs' story is instructive: in a dream world, he probably wanted to paint or be an artist. Instead, he went through the process of becoming a great CEO and then wove his creativity back into computers. You become an expert in your craft first, then weave your creativity back into it. That order matters.

## The View Ladder and Counter-Positioning

Counter-positioning is what moves you from 0-300 views to breaking out:

- **0-300 views**: Only focus on the basics. Ask 10 people: "Why should someone watch you?" Lip-syncing in the mirror is not a good enough reason. Giving three tips on how to get rich when you're not rich isn't either.
- **800-1,700 views**: Most creators get stuck here because of poor ideation. Ideation is the number one skill set of a great creator.
- **1K-3K views**: Hooks, storytelling, pacing. Stop rambling.
- **3K-10K views**: Must understand data and communication tactics. Break videos into "chapters" and mix-and-match winners.
- **10K-50K views**: Data analysis + literary devices + frameworks. You start to realize you can't just rant into the camera.
- **50K+**: Content engine with testing and a team.

At every level, counter-positioning is the underlying skill that separates stuck creators from those who break through.

## Counter-Positioning and Monetization

### The Dethroning Strategy

Counter-position against the biggest creator in your space:

- While they preach "work every single day," position with lifestyle entrepreneurship
- Never repurpose content (they do)
- Keep costs low (they spend millions)
- Outlast with a 5-7 year timeline

### Funnel Counter-Positioning

The traditional "comment X to get my Y" funnel is dying. Counter-positions:

1. **The quiet outreach method**: Never say "buy my product." Instead reach out asking "Are you interested in growing your business or just seeing my videos?" Slowly pitch through DMs. An invitation, not a sales pitch. (Dan Martel uses this approach.)
2. **The paid ads separation method**: Top and middle funnel on organic. Bottom funnel ONLY on paid ads. The biggest creators don't constantly pitch on organic - they use paid for that. You'd get annoyed if they didn't.
3. **The Stories method**: Top and middle funnel on feed. Sales only on Stories and Lives. Your Stories audience is only your top 10% - they are willing to hear you talk about what you're selling. Your feed audience is not.

### Bottom of Funnel Counter-Position

The most effective product promotions are baked into entertainment. A viral story about a personal experience that naturally involves a product gets millions of views. A straightforward "check out this app" gets 5-10K. The product must feel organic - it cannot feel like a paid ad at all.

The best sponsorship integrations follow the same principle: read a biography about someone who kept costs low, then naturally mention a company that helps keep costs low. That's not an ad read. That's a demonstration.

Three principles of great bottom-of-funnel content:

1. Baked into education or entertainment
2. Basic copywriting principles (pain points, tangible benefits, social proof)
3. Must feel organic - cannot feel like a paid ad at all

## Common Mistakes

### The Biggest Positioning Mistakes

1. **Zero differentiation brand pillars**: "My brand pillars are fitness, lifestyle, and entrepreneurship." Meaningless.
2. **Copying big creators**: Their current approach is already played out by the time you replicate it.
3. **Inventing a fake counter-position**: Don't make things up. What do you actually believe that is in juxtaposition with your niche? If you're not goth, don't go goth.
4. **Thinking expertise wins**: There are billion-dollar CEOs doing podcasts getting 50 views.
5. **Self-centered content**: Most creators think people care about their lifestyle. They don't. That's the painful truth.
6. **Rage baiting as counter-position**: Getting views is not the same as building a personal brand. Why would anyone want to become a fan of rage bait?
7. **Changing niches too often**: You lose all your compounding results. Pick something and stay long enough to compound.
8. **Nuancing your takes to death**: Nuance is the death of a great content creator. Take a hard take without qualifying it. Let people debate in comments.

### The Perfectionism Curse

In the content game, perfectionism is a curse. Not a blessing and a curse. Only a curse.

Unlike movies where you make 5-10 and perfection matters, content has feedback loops that change every 24 hours. The speed to feedback is what makes a great content creator. The fastest-growing creators share one trait: a lack of perfectionism.

## Real-World Examples

- **Liquid Death**: Counter-positioned the entire water bottle industry. Founded 2018, climbed to #4 market share with 3% while all major incumbents declined. (source: Aquafina 13%, Evian 2%, Fiji 2%, Smart Water 7% - all fell. Liquid Death went up.)
- **Anti-guru positioning**: Cheap house in Omaha, analytical approach vs. Miami guru lifestyle. Drives a modest car despite being able to afford otherwise - the choice itself is the counter-position.
- **Andrew Huberman**: Science + self-improvement
- **Shelby Sapp**: Female in high-ticket sales niche. Gender alone is counter-position. Three years barely missing three posts a day.
- **Jeff Magnuson**: Object lessons in the fitness niche while everyone lifts and looks hot in mirrors
- **Greg Eisenberg**: Monotonous, analytical, nerdy delivery in business niche
- **David Senra**: Reads biographies and turns them into solo podcasts instead of two-person interview format
- **M&M's blue package**: In the candy aisle at Target, blue stands out against all other colors. Packaging is positioning. It's the concept of YouTube thumbnails applied to retail.
- **Email counter-position**: One email in a crowded inbox that says "Stop. Do not buy from us" - while everyone else is running promotions. They were running a sale the next day.
- **Sam Walton/Walmart**: Beat Kmart by keeping costs low. The same principle applied to creator businesses: low expenses outlast higher-spending competitors.

## Tactical Playbook

### Step 1: Audit Your Niche (Week 1)

1. List the top 10 creators in your niche
2. Input into AI: what they talk about, formats used, values projected
3. Identify the default look, feel, and message of your space
4. Map where they fall on the niche quality quadrant

### Step 2: Find Your Gap (Week 2)

1. Write down your genuine beliefs that oppose the top 10
2. Look at 3-5 niches completely different from yours for format inspiration
3. Identify which of the 5 categories (brand pillars, format, delivery, positioning, credibility) has the widest open gap
4. Pick ONE to go hard on first

### Step 3: Exercise It (Week 3-4)

1. Apply your counter-position to your next 10 posts
2. Track followers-per-view (not total views) on counter-positioned vs. normal content
3. If counter-positioned content gets fewer views but more followers per view, you're winning

### Step 4: Expand (Month 2+)

1. Once one category is dialed, add a second counter-position layer
2. Follow the Amazon blueprint: dominate your pocket before expanding
3. Use the accordion method to test volume with your new positioning

### Clothing as Counter-Position

Three attributes for what you wear:

1. **Pattern interrupt**: Strong contrast from background. Bright blue against neutral. Even holding a pen is a visual pacifier.
2. **Association**: What do you want people to think? Doctor/therapist = scrubs. Finance = suit. Accessories from brands that signal the identity you want to attract.
3. **Conversation starter**: Inside jokes on t-shirts, team jerseys that spark debate, brands that signal identity. If videos average 3,000 views and 15 comments, getting just 3-5 extra comments could be the difference between 3,000 and 5,000 views.

## Key Principles Summary

- Differentiation is survival - standing out is a prerequisite for everything else
- Pattern interruption is the #1 skill in content and in marketing
- Uniqueness beats expertise on social media, every time
- Copying is dead - market cycles move too fast
- Five categories to differentiate: brand pillars, format, delivery, positioning, credibility
- The pendulum swings - read public sentiment and get ahead of it
- Great creators are 60% marketer, 20% showman, 10% teacher, 10% athlete
- Amazon blueprint: dominate your pocket before expanding
- The passion trap: build skill first, fall in love with it later
- Nuance kills great content - take hard takes, let people debate
- Perfectionism is a curse, not a virtue, in content
