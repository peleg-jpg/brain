# Hooks and Retention

80% of hook success does not come from the words you say. It comes from the on-screen text.

## Core Principles

### The 5 Hooks of Every Video

Every single video has five distinct hooks running simultaneously, and a great creator builds **synergy** between all five - not copying or repeating, but complementing each other so the viewer processes multiple layers of information at once.

There are five hooks to every single video, and a great content creator creates synergy between all five, not actually copying all five.

**1. Verbal Hook** - The first three sentences you say out loud. This is where you open the curiosity loop and signal who the video is for.

The verbal hook does not need to be fast or copied from others. It needs to open a curiosity loop and signal either a dream outcome or a pain point so the right viewer self-selects into watching. You do not have to say it fast. You can pause. The main goal is opening a curiosity loop. For example: saying "some of the best creators" signals the video is for people who want to be among the best creators.

**2. On-Screen Text Hook** - The text overlay that appears at frame zero. This is the area where the most impact lives, and the critical rule is it must complement the verbal hook, not repeat it.

The on-screen text should be in correlation with what was said verbally - covering something from later in the video, or hitting a different psychological angle that the verbal hook did not address.

**3. Visual Hook** - What the viewer sees visually in the first frame. This can be as simple as a hood up, a 0.5 cam angle, holding a pen, or a microphone in shot.

The visual hook serves a defensive function - making sure you "belong on the platform." Bad visual hooks include: DJI Osmo camera filters that look "off," bubbly highlighted captions, anything that screams over-produced.

The goal is simple: basic default captions, a basic system font. Look like you belong on the platform natively.

**4. Format Hook** - The format itself can be a hook if people have not seen it before. A reaction video, a whiteboard lesson, a split screen, an object lesson - the novelty of the format creates its own hook rate independent of what you say.

Formats have a hook rate in themselves if people have never seen them before. A reaction video in a niche where nobody had done that format before generates a high hook rate even before a word is spoken.

**5. Caption Hook** - The small text at the bottom of the video. This can serve as a secondary on-screen text hook or a passive CTA.

The bottom caption can be used to say something simple like "follow for more content strategy" - functioning as a passive CTA without interrupting the main delivery. It can also act as a second on-screen text hook to accomplish a separate goal.

### Hooks Should Complement, Not Repeat

One of the most emphatic principles in this framework: if your on-screen text says the same thing as your verbal hook, you are wasting the viewer's cognitive bandwidth. The human brain processes around 70+ pieces of information in the first two seconds of a video - use that bandwidth to your advantage by delivering different context in each layer.

Never repeat your on-screen text in your verbal hook. If you do this for one week, you will likely see views go up by around 20%.

### Context Coverage - The Advanced Hook Strategy

An advanced concept: the on-screen text and verbal hook together should "cover" as much of the video's content as possible - signaling different parts of the video to create multiple curiosity threads simultaneously.

This is called context coverage. Whatever is covered in the on-screen text and verbal hook should try to span as much of the full video content as possible.

Practical example: imagine a video timeline from 0 to 60 seconds covering three topics - "3 tips to garden better" in the first third, some story in the middle, and "a mathematical breakdown of profit margins" at the end.

Instead of both the on-screen text and verbal hook saying "3 tips to garden better," split coverage:

- **On-screen text**: "the math behind 90% profit margins" (covers the END of the video)
- **Verbal hook**: "3 tips to garden better" (covers the BEGINNING of the video)

This approach covers 66% of the video inside the hook alone.

The genius is that it does not just boost hook rate - it drives watch time too. The viewer who stopped for the on-screen text about profit margins is now watching through the gardening tips to get there. The viewer who stopped for the verbal hook about gardening tips is also curious about the math. A "curiosity bridge" forms in the middle where the viewer holds multiple open threads simultaneously.

People usually get one or the other, but a great hook also drives watch time.

### Pain + Dream Outcome + Curiosity - The Three Angles

Every hook should hit at least one, ideally all three, of these psychological levers:

**Pain** - Address your viewer's biggest, most specific fear. Not "losing money" (too vague). Rich people fear paying too much in taxes. Single people fear being single their whole life. Young men fear addiction. Barbecue competitors fear showing up to the state fair with small carrots they grew all summer.

**Dream Outcome** - What does your ideal viewer want more than anything? Be specific. Not "$10,000 a month" (overplayed). Barbecue competitors want to stand at the grand stand with the blue ribbon around their neck. Career people want that specific promotion. Fitness people want visible abs by summer.

**Curiosity** - Add a secret sauce element that prevents the viewer from leaving.

A full example that hits all three: "If you are sick and tired of showing up to the state fair with small little carrots that you grew all summer, such a waste of time, and instead you want to be at the top, you want to be on the grand stand with that blue ribbon around your neck with the fattest carrot ever made, there are three simple tips. And if you want to win your state fair without having to spend a fortune [curiosity]..." Put all three together and you have a strong hook.

This requires real research on your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile). Your conversion at the back end of your video starts with your hook. You need to understand what your viewers hate and fear at a specific level. Not "losing money" - that is too vague.

### The Hook Formula (Build-a-Video Method)

A complete fill-in-the-blank formula for building a video from scratch, starting with the hook:

**Hook structure**: "If you are an X [person], and you are tired of [pain point], and you want [dream outcome]... then you need to watch this video."

Step by step process:

1. Ask ChatGPT: who is my ideal viewer profile? Describe them perfectly - age, interests, fears, desires.
2. Ask: what is their number one pain point? It has to be unique and widely observed.
3. Ask: what is their dream outcome? Not just money, not just a spouse. Be specific and unique.
4. Combine: "If you are a gardener and you are tired of your lettuce having dry leaves and you want moist leaves that people have no other choice but to tip you hundreds of dollars at the local farmer's market - then you need to watch this video."
5. Bridge: "Then you need to hear the story" - a 20-second story
6. Re-hook: "That story matters, but there is more to it."
7. Three punchy tips
8. CTA: "Follow for more content like this."

Keep this around 90 seconds. Do not get caught up on the time.

### One Word Can Target a Whole Different Demographic

Hooks are fragile and precise. Changing a single word can shift who watches.

One word in a hook targets a whole different demographic. Even the emotional undertone - the way you say it, the descriptors, the dream outcomes, the pain points - will get a completely different audience with the same sentence.

A real example: opening a video with 45 seconds about a pop musician attracted thousands of music followers the creator did not want. If the creator had started by speaking directly about content strategy, the follower quality would have been completely different.

Who you bring in through a hook is not a neutral act - they are not all created equal.

## On-Screen Text Hooks

### 5 Principles for On-Screen Text Copywriting

A five-principle framework for on-screen text:

**1. Curiosity** - Are you alluding to something later in the video? Use the on-screen text to signal a future payoff that the verbal hook does not mention.

**2. Pain Points** - Think about your ideal viewer profile. What do they absolutely hate? What are their biggest fears? Address that directly in the on-screen text.

**3. Dream Outcome** - What does your ideal viewer want more than anything? Be specific - not generic "$10,000 a month" which is overplayed.

**4. Tangibility** - Make the benefit something the viewer can physically imagine experiencing. Can they hold it? Can they grab it? Can they smell it? Can they see it?

**5. The Whisper Technique** - Use parentheses or quotation marks to add a secondary message that either builds curiosity or handles an objection (risk reversal). Think about the number one thing that might prevent someone from watching this video and get ahead of it. For example: "not much time required" addresses a time objection before the viewer can raise it.

### Formatting Rules for On-Screen Text

**Two lines maximum** - Three lines overwhelm the viewer.

**Keep it on screen for 10+ seconds** - Most creators cut on-screen text after 2-3 seconds, which is the platform default. Stretch it out so viewers can read it, process it, look at the visuals, and come back.

Many creators cut it way too fast - two to three seconds, which is the native default on Instagram and TikTok. Stretching it to 10 seconds or even the full video gives people the pacing to read and understand while also listening.

**Only native or luxury fonts** - No fancy, bubbly, or highlighted fonts. Use the native font or a luxury font with beautiful typography.

**No function words** - Strip out I, you, he, she, they, in, on, at, and, but, a, some, is, are, not, up. Every word should be a keyword, buzzword, proper noun, or verb. Function words are words that do not matter, and they kill retention and hook rate.

**Break lines by structure, not character limit** - Do not let text auto-wrap when it hits maximum width. Break at a meaningful point in the sentence.

### The Art of Digital Reading

The backbone of content is writing. People read online differently than they read books. They take screenshots and look for keywords. The more function words in on-screen text, the harder it is for someone to quickly understand what the video is about.

A real performance analysis shows the pattern clearly: the worst-performing videos had over 50% function words in their on-screen text and duplicated the same word. The best-performing videos had around 33% function words with no word duplication.

**Pattern interrupt tools** for on-screen text: parentheses, quotation marks, bold numbers. These break the visual monotony as someone scrolls. When someone is scrolling and sees sentence after sentence, a sudden set of parentheses or quotation marks creates a visual stop.

### A/B Testing On-Screen Text with Trial Reels

A scientific method for finding the best on-screen text:

Take the same video and create 10 versions with different on-screen text - different colors, sizes, placements, fonts, and wordings. Post them all as trial reels. The only metric that matters is the reels skip rate (hook rate) - how many people watched past the first 3 seconds.

Important note: Meta added a visual duplicate detector that suppresses videos that look too similar. Changes need to be significant - move text from top to bottom, switch from blue to red, change the font. Slight tweaks will not work.

### A Practical On-Screen Text Process

A three-step process works well:

1. Upload your recorded video to an AI-assisted tool
2. Analyze - generate 10 on-screen text options
3. Take the two favorites and combine the most unique element from each

AI-generated options may be about 80% better than what most creators are doing on their own. But combining the two favorites and extracting the most unique element from each tends to produce the best result.

## Super Hooks

### What is a Super Hook?

The super hook is the second sentence after your opening hook. It answers the single most important question the viewer has: "Why should I listen to you?"

The first sentence tells viewers what the video is about in a creative and hooky way. The second sentence is the super hook - it answers why the viewer should keep listening.

Think of it like a landing page. Landing pages have social proof, testimonials, statistics, reviews - they tell the buyer why they should buy. In videos, you need to tell people why they should listen.

If you think you can just make a video and get views without earning the viewer's attention - without telling them why they should listen to you - that is a shortcut that becomes a long cut that does not work.

The super hook is likely the number one reason creators get stuck in the 200-800 view range.

### 5 Types of Super Hooks

**1. Effort Condensed** - Show that you put in massive effort so the viewer does not have to. The effort itself is the proof of credibility.

Example: "I spent 10,000 hours reading about cults. Here is what I learned."

**2. Failure** - Use your failures as credibility. People trust someone who has been through it.

Example: "I started and failed five businesses and all five businesses had one thing in common."

**3. Credibility Arbitrage** - Borrow credibility from famous people, statistics, quotes, or books. You do not have to have personal achievements - you can pull from others.

Example: "I just heard this quote from Huberman..." This is where the super hook does not have to be about you. You can say "what I learned from reading 100 books" or "listening to a podcast" or "60 minutes from a Diary of a CEO podcast boiled into 60 seconds." It just has to be credibility.

**4. Shared Observable Emotion** - Reference a specific emotion or experience that only insiders in your niche know about. This creates an instant "this person gets it" reaction.

Example: If all top gardeners feel the extreme pain of pulling up a carrot and seeing it withered at the bottom, referencing that specific feeling signals deep niche knowledge.

**5. Unique Mentorship** - Pull from unique experiences others do not have access to.

Example: "Day one of learning from my billionaire boss."

### Super Hook Best Practices

- Should appear in 80% of your videos
- Keep 2-3 different super hooks in rotation so you are not saying the same thing every time
- Say it without being arrogant - nonchalant delivery works better than flashy
- Test different angles, speeds, and numbers
- If you have never done anything noteworthy, go DO something first before trying to build a following

The principle here is important: if you have done nothing to earn a super hook, to earn someone's view, the better long-term move is to make some content but prioritize going and doing something that would earn real credibility.

A story that illustrates this: trying content creation for a year and flopping, then spending a year building a business, finding a mentor, working with large creators - not posting content, just developing real results. When coming back to content, the super hooks were authentic and the compounding worked.

Real-world super hooks in practice: a bodybuilder's physique is their super hook. An info product person uses their income. A luxury car is a super hook. A former Nike marketing director uses the Nike logo behind them. A wrestling coach uses "Division 1 wrestler" as credibility.

### How to Build a Super Hook If You Have Not Done Anything Yet

Options beyond personal achievement:

- Condense effort: "I spent 200 hours researching this so you do not have to"
- Borrow credibility: "According to a Harvard study..." or "What I learned from reading 100 books"
- Reference shared insider experiences that only your niche understands
- Use failure as proof: "I tried 5 approaches and 4 of them failed"

## Curiosity Loops and Bridges

### Curiosity Loop

The simplest retention mechanism. You open a question in the viewer's mind and do not answer it immediately.

Example: asking "what are the five things?" at the start creates an instant, simple curiosity loop. The viewer is now waiting for the five things.

### Curiosity Gap

The space between the hook and the payoff where you tell a story or build context instead of giving the answer right away.

A curiosity gap means not giving the secret sauce quite yet. Instead, tell a quick story to get retention up.

Practical example: You promise "three tips for moist carrots" in the hook, but before delivering those tips, you tell a 20-30 second story about winning the state fair. The viewer stays because they are waiting for the tips, but the story keeps them engaged through the middle.

### Curiosity Bridge

The curiosity bridge connects the hook to the payoff. When done right alongside context coverage, it creates a section where viewers are holding multiple open curiosity threads simultaneously.

If you drive retention up through the curiosity bridge - maybe by talking about mistakes, what people usually do, or sharing a story - people are waiting for the three tips and also waiting for the mathematical breakdown, because you signaled both in the on-screen text and the hook. Multiple threads keep the viewer pinned.

### Storytelling as Retention - The Never-Ending Question Method

A useful framework for storytelling (referenced from Colin and Samir):

The story is over when someone quits asking a question. The great story ends when the viewer no longer has questions. Introduce a problem or a question, draw out some curiosity or context, do not answer it right away, answer it, and then immediately reopen another question.

The structure: introduce a question - build curiosity - do not answer yet - finally answer - immediately reopen a new question or piece of conflict - draw out more - answer and end. The viewer never has a resting point where all questions are resolved, so they never have a natural exit from the video.

Watch time is one of the three most important metrics (alongside sends per view and saves per view), and great storytelling is what drives watch time. It actually does not matter if content gets shared or saved - if someone watches it for a long time, it goes very far.

## Retention Techniques

### The Re-loop

After delivering the climax (the main payoff the viewer was waiting for), instead of ending the video, re-hook them with a single word: "but."

Give them the climax, then say "but that is not all" or "but there is more to it." This is extremely simple and extremely slept on. It sends the viewer into "body number two" - a second piece of advice that overdelivers on the original promise. It extends watch time and increases the chance of getting a follow from people who feel they got more than expected.

Make sure the second body is unique if the goal is followers.

### Transcript Editing for Retention

Most people have 20% fluff in their communication - repeating themselves, going on tangents, filler sounds. Cutting this fluff directly translates to better retention.

Go into CapCut on desktop. Upload the video. Click transcript - it appears in the sidebar. Go through and cut anytime you repeat yourself or say something not attached to the main point. This takes an extra 15 minutes and typically produces around 20% more views.

Only ask yourself two questions while editing the transcript:

1. "Did I already say this?" - If so, cut it.
2. "Is this attached to the main point?" - If not, cut it.

### Speed Up to 1.1x (or 1.05x)

Speeding up your video slightly decreases the churn rate because viewers do not get bored as easily. Critical detail: only do this in CapCut, not TikTok or other editors, because those will distort your voice.

A refined version is 1.05x combined with a pitch-up voice effect for a "sweetening" of the audio - inspired by how music producers pitch recordings up to hit a certain tonal range. 1.05x gets that sweet spot where talking has a subtle audio sweetening without going too far.

### Shikaki (Nudge Design)

Shikaki is the Japanese art of changing behavior through design. Practical applications for content: putting a basketball hoop over a trash can in a fourth-grade classroom makes kids more likely to throw things away. The principle applies to video.

Can you change someone's behavior through design?

Practical applications:

- Put "hold for 2x speed" text on screen to get people to hold and watch longer
- Place a blue heart emoji sticker exactly where the like button is (subconsciously prompting likes)
- Place an airplane emoji over the send button
- Place a bookmark over the save button
- Put a timer across the bottom of the screen to make people want to watch until it runs out

So many creators care about fancy edits, zooms, and production - that is getting overrated. Behavioral design through nudges often works better.

### Hook Rate vs Watch Time - The Connection

Many creators treat these as separate metrics, but they are deeply connected. Watch time is heavily dependent on hook rate. If the hook does not work, you get a high percentage of 1-second views, and then your watch time data is not meaningful.

To analyze both, use the Edits app (Instagram's editing tool) which shows a retention graph. Look for:

- Large dips: if you dropped 5% between seconds 15 and 19, something in that range was wrong.
- Large holds: if you only lost 1% over 14 seconds, you had exceptional retention on that part.

### The Eight Psychological Triggers

From the book "Cashvertising," eight psychological triggers drive retention and engagement when woven into content:

1. **Enjoyment of life / life extension** - "Eat these three carrots to have healthier joints in your thirties"
2. **Enjoyment of food or beverages** - "This recipe for the best pizza you have ever had"
3. **Freedom from fear, pain, or danger**
4. **Sexual companionship**
5. **Comfortable living conditions**
6. **Superior/winning / keeping up with the Joneses** - "Make this recipe when friends come over to signal high status"
7. **Care and protection of loved ones** - "Make this natural smoothie to help your mom avoid cancer"
8. **Social approval** - "Make this recipe at the family dinner so everyone sees you as the backbone"

## Video Structure Frameworks

### Hook - Explain - Illustrate - Apply

A primary content framework, borrowed from public speaking. Content is just public speaking. There are formulas in public speaking that have worked for generations.

**1. Explain** - Three sub-principles:

- Extremely simply (fifth grade reading level)
- Uniquely (novel information or framing)
- With personality (not reading a script)

Complexity is one of the worst issues in content. Fancy words in on-screen text do not perform. Clear and simple wins.

**2. Illustrate** - Use a literary device to make the explanation stick:

- Metaphors, examples, statistics, quotes, books, listicles, step-by-step processes

Illustrating re-emphasizes what was just explained because you want content that sticks.

**3. Apply** - Give tactical, specific, unique advice. Not "do better push-ups" but "do a 10 by 3 where you roll your elbow in because it triggers your biceps and hold your breath for four seconds on the way down."

Tactical specificity is the difference between actionable and vague advice.

### Four-Stage Video Structure (with Timing)

A granular version with specific second counts:

Three seconds hook, five to eight seconds explain, 20 to 30 seconds illustration (story, metaphor, example, quote, statistic, case study), then application and next action steps.

### The Perfect Video Anatomy (Full Breakdown)

The most complete video structure:

1. **Hook** (3-5 seconds) - Pain + benefit + curiosity
2. **Super hook** (1-2 sentences) - Credibility sentence
3. **Curiosity gap** (20-30 seconds) - A story that builds anticipation without revealing the secret
4. **Climax** (the payoff) - Deliver the promised tips/secret
5. **Re-loop** - "But that is not all..."
6. **Body two** - A second piece of advice that overdelivers
7. **CTA** - Follow for more / comment for more

### Build-a-Video (Complete Template)

The step-by-step video-building method:

1. **Hook Part A** (pain): "If you are a gardener and you are tired of your lettuce having dry leaves..."
2. **Hook Part B** (dream outcome): "...and you want moist leaves that people tip you hundreds of dollars at the farmer's market"
3. **Bridge**: "Then you need to hear the story"
4. **Story** (20 seconds): Quick story from history, personal experience, movies, books
5. **Re-hook**: "That story matters, but there is more to it"
6. **Three tips**: Step one, step two, step three - keep them punchy
7. **CTA**: "Follow for more content like this"

Target: ~90 seconds, but do not stress about time.

## Common Mistakes

### Repeating On-Screen Text in Verbal Hook

The number one hook mistake. You are wasting the 70+ pieces of information the brain can process in 2 seconds.

### Using Function Words in On-Screen Text

Words like "I," "you," "the," "and," "in" waste visual real estate. Over 50% function words = a poorly optimized hook.

### Cutting On-Screen Text Too Fast

2-3 seconds is too short. Keep it on screen for 10+ seconds. The viewer needs time to read, look at visuals, then re-read.

### No Super Hook

If you are stuck at 200-800 views, you probably do not have a credibility sentence. The viewer has no reason to keep watching you specifically.

### Vague Pain Points and Dream Outcomes

"Losing money" is too vague. "Showing up to the state fair with small carrots you grew all summer" is specific and emotionally resonant.

### Not Researching Your ICP

Hooks written without deep understanding of what your viewer actually fears and desires will always underperform. Research on Reddit, polls, comments, and FAQs first.

### Fancy/Bubbly Fonts and Over-Editing

Bad visual hooks. Anything that makes you look like you do not belong on the platform natively will suppress your hook rate.

### Copying Other People's Hooks Directly

Hooks from other niches will not work because each niche has different viewing behaviors and psychology. Women often prefer aspirational/lifestyle content. Men often prefer status/educational content. The same hook in two different niches performs completely differently.

### Using AI for Scripting

Scripting in general destroys naturalness. Use an outline and record in one cut. AI should be used for research, A/B testing hooks, and cross-posting - never for writing the actual script.

## Tactical Playbook

1. Create all 5 hooks for every video (verbal, on-screen text, visual, format, caption) and ensure synergy between them
2. Never repeat your on-screen text in your verbal hook - complement it with different context coverage
3. Strip function words from on-screen text. Aim for 6 words or fewer, all keywords/buzzwords/verbs
4. Keep on-screen text visible for 10+ seconds, not the default 2-3 seconds
5. Use the whisper technique (parentheses/quotes) for risk reversals and curiosity builders
6. Include a super hook (credibility sentence) in 80% of your videos - keep 2-3 in rotation
7. Use the pain + dream outcome + curiosity formula for your verbal hook
8. Research your ICP's specific fears and desires on Reddit, YouTube comments, and story polls before writing any hook
9. Use the context coverage strategy to make your hook drive both hook rate AND watch time
10. Build a curiosity bridge between the hook and the payoff - do not give away the answer immediately
11. Add a re-loop after the climax ("but that is not all...") to extend watch time
12. Edit transcripts in CapCut desktop to cut 20% fluff
13. Speed up to 1.05-1.1x in CapCut (not other editors - they distort voice)
14. Apply Shikaki design - blue heart over the like button, airplane over send, bookmark over save
15. A/B test hooks via trial reels - the only metric that matters is skip rate (hook rate)
16. Use the Edits app to analyze the retention graph for dips and holds
17. Follow the four-stage structure: 3s hook, 5-8s explain, 20-30s illustrate, then apply
18. Do not script - use an outline and record in one cut for natural delivery

## Key Principles (Condensed)

- 80% of hook success lives in on-screen text, not what you say verbally.
- Five hooks exist in every video - verbal, on-screen text, visual, format, and caption. Build synergy between all five.
- Never repeat on-screen text in your verbal hook. Cover different parts of the video with each.
- Context coverage: split the video's content between your two primary hooks to drive both hook rate and watch time simultaneously.
- Super hook = credibility sentence. It answers "why should I listen to you?" Appears in 80% of videos.
- Pain + dream outcome + curiosity is the hook formula. Research your ICP's specific fears before writing any hook.
- The story is over when someone quits asking a question. Never let all threads resolve at once.
- Transcript editing cuts 20% fluff. Speed adjustment reduces churn. Shikaki design nudges behavior.
- Watch time and hook rate are connected - a bad hook makes watch time data irrelevant.
