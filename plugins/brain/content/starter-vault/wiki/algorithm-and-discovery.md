# Algorithm and Discovery

Staying out of low-view stagnation requires consistent viral comment activity - daily engagement in high-traffic content is part of the system.

How platforms decide what to push, how content gets discovered, and why most "algorithm hacks" miss the point. The framework here is rooted in mass psychology and data - not hashtags, posting times, or feature tricks. The algorithm is not a machine to be gamed. It is a crowd to be moved.

## Core Principles

### The Algorithm is a Stadium

The foundational metaphor: the algorithm is essentially a massive stadium. The question is not "how do I trick the algorithm" but "could I say something to get a reaction from 100,000 people in a stadium?" If you were standing there with a microphone and two minutes, could you make them laugh? Make them cry? Make them uncomfortable? Would you know what you were doing?

This framing is called **Mass Psychology** or **Algorithmic Psychology** - the ability to understand what resonates with large groups of people.

### The Viral Comment Test

Want to know if you are cut out for content? This is the diagnostic:

1. Go into the comments of viral videos
2. Try to become the most liked comment

If you can go into a viral video and become the top comment, that is a strong signal you understand mass psychology, stadium psychology, and algorithmic psychology. The skill is rare.

One useful benchmark: try going into a high-traffic celebrity post and becoming the most liked comment. If you can resonate with a crowd that is already gathered, you can build your own crowd.

### 99% of Algorithm Tips Are Bullshit

Scrolling content advice, the overwhelming majority falls into useless categories: how to edit, which hashtags to use, when to post, what the platform CEO just said. Most of it does not matter.

What does NOT matter (for most creators):

- Hashtags
- Time of day posting
- Specific editing tricks
- Copying other creators' exact tactics
- "Use this feature" or "turn this setting on"

What DOES matter:

- The topic/idea of the video (by far the number 1 factor)
- Your uniqueness and counter-position
- Comment engagement
- Understanding mass psychology
- Hook rate and watch time

If your algorithm feed is feeding you tips about which feature to turn on or which three hooks go viral, you are consuming the least useful part of the internet.

### Three Major Problems with Modern Content

Content creators are essentially beginners in the grand scheme of marketing. The analogy: whenever something new enters the world, the first few years the quality of participants is terrible. Early Fortnite players weren't good. Early social media creators aren't good at marketing.

World-class marketers, looking at social media content, identify three problems:

1. **No incremental data improvements**: Nobody does this. Every other marketing medium - email, paid ads, movie trailers, TV - makes adjustments off data. Content creators don't. They shoot from the hip.
2. **No psychology behind it**: Creators make the type of content they want to make, with no research and no study of mass human psychology.
3. **No strategic connection**: There is no connection from one video to the next. Paid ads have a connected sequence: ad to landing page to email to website to retargeted ad. Content videos exist in isolation.

Fixing any one of the three puts you in the top 1% of content.

### Algorithms Find What You Didn't Know You Needed

A strong counterargument to "education content is dead because of LLMs": algorithms find information you did not know you needed. This is why information content will never go away.

You have to know what to search for in ChatGPT. But algorithms surface what you need before you know you need it. This is why social media will never be replaced by AI search for discovery.

The implication for creators: if you are making content someone could have found from a basic ChatGPT search, you will get run over. You have to be innovative and unique. Copycats will die. Original thinkers will thrive because they create the information algorithms surface.

## Content Lifecycle: How Discovery Works

### The Distribution Funnel

Content moves through stages:

1. **Followers first**: Your content gets shown to your existing followers
2. **Follower engagement signals**: If followers engage (watch, like, comment, share, save), the algorithm reads this as a quality signal
3. **Wider distribution**: Content gets pushed to non-followers in explore/FYP
4. **Compounding**: More followers = more initial views = more engagement signals = more distribution = more profile visits = more followers

If your followers are bought or from the wrong niche, they will not engage. This tells the algorithm it is a bad video. This is the mechanism behind 300-view jail.

### The 300-View Jail Mechanism

A complete "worst enemy content strategy" explains how 300-view jail works:

1. Start with paid ads to get 10,000 followers who don't actually care about you
2. Post a video - it gets shown to these fake followers first
3. They don't watch because they were bought, not earned
4. Algorithm reads this as "terrible video"
5. Video never gets sent to non-followers
6. You're stuck at 100-300 views permanently

How to escape - the single easiest method is to understand the right topic or idea:

1. Go to Reddit. Find subreddits in your niche.
2. Sort by "top of the month" (not hot, not new, not controversial)
3. Copy the entire conversation - post and all replies
4. Paste into ChatGPT and ask: "Give me the two sides that are arguing"
5. Identify the sentiment and exact vocabulary they use
6. Make your video positioned in the middle, then take a side at the end
7. Use their exact words and vernacular

Done well, this approach reliably produces first-time outlier performance.

### Algorithm Momentum

When a video goes viral, it creates momentum for subsequent posts. A client posted a viral carousel that hit 300,000 views with 98% non-followers. The low-performing video format posted 12 hours later - which normally underperformed - became a 3x outlier.

The mechanism: viewers who saw the viral video now have the creator in their feed rotation. The next video benefits from this residual exposure.

Have you ever seen a funny piece of content, scrolled a few times, and then seen that same person again without ever having followed them? That is algorithm momentum.

**The reverse is devastating**: Going quiet for a month can crash a baseline from 50,000 to 7,000 views. Other creators moved into viewers' top rotation slots during that absence. You have to rebuild your way back into the rotation.

**Strategic application**: Balance top-of-funnel (viral) with bottom-of-funnel (sales). Go viral to push viewership up, then trickle down with educational content, then sales content. This is how you get a 1,000-view bottom-of-funnel video to 10-15,000 views.

### Stories Are Algorithmic Too

IG stories and TikTok stories are ordered at the top of the screen based on who you engage with most. There is still an algorithmic sequence.

A tactic: wake up early and post a story asking followers to send a specific emoji if they are also up. Everyone who responds at that hour sends the emoji back. This signals high engagement to the algorithm, pushing the story forward in the queue.

Result: when you promote your product on stories, instead of 40,000 views you get 80,000 views.

Every time someone replies, reply back with a short message. This deepens the engagement signal.

## Total Addressable Market (TAM)

TAM is one of the most important concepts for understanding why some content gets views and some does not.

### The Family Feud Method

Every video has a TAM - the total number of people who could possibly care about the topic. The Family Feud analogy: the question is "what is the best food in the world?" If the top answer is pizza at 54 points, pizza has the highest TAM. Clams would be dead last.

### The TAM Deception

Many creators think their videos are underperforming when they have actually maxed out their TAM. If you make a video for "ladies at Arizona State who are interested in Pilates," that is only about a 20,000-person TAM. Getting 2,000 views on that video means you reached 10% of your TAM - that is actually a phenomenal video.

A client in the career/job hack space made a video about conference networking playbooks. It flopped. A virality consultant identified the issue: not very many people have observed that event or circumstance. The fix was to talk about work culture instead - everyone has observed work culture, job interviews, and trying to get a promotion. That is going up TAM.

### Going Up TAM vs. Down TAM

- **High TAM** = broader topics, more views, less targeted followers
- **Low TAM** = niche topics, fewer views, but more loyal followers

One client spent months getting 300 views on AI + Airbnb content until they went higher TAM - shifting to general travel experiences. The channel cracked when a "Greece in the United States" video hit 1.7 million views.

An important warning: virality is not always good. A virality consultant noted it is actually harder to build a loyal following over time when you chase viral breadth.

### Using AI for TAM Analysis

Before posting, evaluate your TAM with AI:

- "Out of 100 people in my niche, how many deeply observe this?"
- If the answer is low, challenge yourself to go higher TAM
- Many creators have videos actually doing reasonably well but are deceiving themselves about the ceiling

### The Ideation Hierarchy

Ideas are the primary driver of content performance. Hooks, editing, and production matter far less than topic selection.

Recommended time allocation for a one-hour content session:

- 40% ideation (scraping competitors, Reddit, YouTube, data analysis)
- 20% growth tactics (trial reels, comment farming, ManyChat)
- 15% strategy (top/middle/bottom funnel balance)
- 10% hooks
- 5% scripting/storytelling
- 5% editing
- 5% copywriting practice

Four ideation frameworks:

1. **Reddit scraping**: Subreddits sorted by top of month, paste into ChatGPT, find the main conversation
2. **Niche-only TikTok**: Follow 5-10 people in your niche, save outliers, but check if it is an outlier for THEM (500K views means nothing if they average 2M)
3. **Internal data**: What performed two weeks ago? New hook, new format, new illustration on same topic
4. **Top 5 competitor comments**: What questions are they NOT answering? Create content that answers those and start drawing some of their audience.

## Trial Reels Strategy

Trial reels are one of the greatest and most underutilized opportunities for creators.

### The Duplicate Detector

Meta added a visual duplicate detector that suppresses content that looks too similar. You can no longer spam your most viral video. Instead:

1. Take one video and create 10 variations with **different on-screen text** - different colors, sizes, placements, fonts, wordings
2. Post up to 20 trial reels per day
3. The ONLY metric that matters on trial reels is **skip rate / hook rate** - not likes, sends, comments, or watch time
4. Find the lowest skip rate and move that version to your main page

Likes, sends, comments, and watch time do not matter on trial reels. Only skip rate.

### The B-Roll One-Liner Method

A particularly effective trial reel strategy:

1. Go into your photo album, take 20 photos
2. Animate these photos with a tool like VO3 (6-7 seconds each)
3. Mine for one-liners: Reddit, ChatGPT, your comments, YouTube podcast comments in your niche
4. Paste all sources into ChatGPT: "Give me 20 one-liners that match my niche from these pieces of content"
5. Put those one-liners on the 20 B-roll videos
6. Post as trial reels daily (up to 20/day)
7. Look for outliers - a baseline of 500 views with one hitting 5,000
8. Analyze the outlier: What was the buzzword? The emotion? The pain point?
9. Then make a full-effort video about that topic

Sometimes the trial reel itself is not quite strong enough to move to the main page, but the underlying consensus it reveals - the buzzword or emotion that drove engagement - is the signal for what to produce next.

### Testing Fields Beyond Trial Reels

Trial reels are one testing ground. Others include:

- **TikTok burner account**: Make a video, create two hooks and two on-screen texts, post both simultaneously. After two hours, see which has the best hook rate. Move the winner to main.
- **Instagram story polls**: "What of these four video concepts would you want to see?" If 100 people vote bicep curls over abs, make the bicep curls video.
- **Twitter/X**: Post thoughts and ideas. Analyze top-performing hooks and topics, turn winners into short-form and long-form content.
- **Yapping into trial reels**: 10-20 times a day, observe which has highest watch time, move the winner topic to a produced video.

The math on testing: Without testing, improving 1% per day, you reach 10K views in 7.5 months. With A/B testing that finds just 5% better content, plus 1% daily improvement, you reach 10K views in 39 days - about six months faster.

### Posting from Edits vs. Trial Reels

Posting directly from the Edits app straight onto your profile is currently outperforming the trial-reel-then-move workflow for many creators. The Instagram Edits app is getting boosted reach as a startup push. Process: add on-screen text, trim a little, turn speed up to 1.1x, post from Edits onto IG.

## What Signals Matter

### The Signal Hierarchy

Based on platform data and IG CEO statements, here is the ranking of what matters:

1. **Watch time / Hook rate** (most important - they go together)
2. **Send rate / Shares** (IG CEO confirmed top 3)
3. **Comments** (strongest growth signal)
4. **Save rate** (indicates unique value)
5. **Follower conversion** (followers per view)

### Understanding Each Metric

**Save rate**: Comes down to unique value. People don't save funny things. They save recipes, swipe file material, tips and tricks they want to come back to later. But they only save if the content is unique.

**Send rate**: Ask yourself: "Does this embody who my ideal viewer is enough that they want to share it?" People put on their story who they want to signal themselves as. Content that insults the viewer will not get shared.

**Watch time**: Heavily dependent on hook rate. If the hook fails, you get a flood of 1% views and watch time becomes irrelevant. Use the Edits app retention graph to find large dips (bad sections) and large holds (great sections).

**Hook rate**: The gateway to everything else. A great hook drives watch time, which drives all other metrics.

### Communal Language for Sends

Instead of individual language, use communal language to drive shares:

- Instead of "I went to this hotel" - say "Meet-in-bae's honeymoon getaway" (gets shared with a spouse)
- Instead of "My daily lifting split" - say "Me and the boys push, pull, split to get shredded in our winter arc" (gets shared in the group chat)

**Ideal Group Profile (IGP)**: Not just an ICP (ideal customer profile), but an ideal GROUP profile. Visualize the exact people, the name of the group chat, what they talk about. Write it on a piece of paper and put it behind the camera.

Three emotional triggers for group sharing:

1. Blow their mind
2. Piss them off (use carefully)
3. Make them laugh

### Comments Are King

Comment engagement is the strongest actionable signal. A systematic comment farming approach:

**Step 1: Reply to every comment with a question**

- Original comment: 1 comment
- Your reply with a question: 2 comments
- Their response: 3 comments
- 10 comments becomes 25 comments - that signals a 250% better video to the algorithm.

**Step 2: Farm haters**
Two phrases that reliably provoke more comments from hostile commenters:

- "Is this AI?"
- "Get out of my DMs bro"

These two phrases make it nearly impossible for someone to not comment back. A chain interaction from one hater comment can generate 9 comments.

**The math**: Going from 10 comments to 40 comments is 400% - the algorithm reads that as a 400% better video and extends its distribution. Even accounting for imperfect correlation, expect roughly 75% more views, meaning 1,000 views becomes closer to 3,000.

**Important**: This is NOT rage baiting. Creating deliberately outrageous takes to attract controversy is a different strategy with different consequences. The hater farming technique applies only to hostile commenters who come to you - not to manufacturing controversy in your own content.

### Four Tactical Ways to Drive Comments

Beyond comment farming:

1. **Leave out a small piece of information**: People will comment asking about it, or provide the answer themselves. Short form only has 60 seconds - it is completely fine to not answer everything.

2. **Take a hard take with no nuance**: Not rage bait - just your actual opinion stated firmly. Nuance is the death of a great content creator. Let people debate in comments.

3. **Use communal language**: "You and your partner" or "you and your team" instead of "you." People tag their partners or teammates.

4. **Use repeated themes**: A recurring prop, a running joke, a familiar setting. Superfans comment about recurring elements. Three extra comments from repeated themes is 30% more comments and 30% more views.

### Saves and Shares - The "Algo" Folder Strategy

Creating a saved folder named "algo" for friends' content, sending videos to a friend with just the word "algo," and constructively engaging in comments on a friend's videos - these all drive save and share signals that boost those videos.

## Shikaki - Design That Drives Action

Shikaki is the Japanese art of shaping behavior through design. Real-world examples: a basketball hoop over a trash can so kids throw things away, footprints in grocery aisles to direct foot traffic.

Applied to content:

- Place a blue heart emoji over the like icon to prompt likes
- Place an airplane emoji over the send icon to prompt shares
- Place a bookmark over the save icon to prompt saves
- Add "hold for 2x speed" text to increase watch time
- Put a timer across the bottom of the screen to make people watch longer

Many creators focus on fancy editing and zooms. Shikaki asks a different question: can you change someone's behavior through design?

## The Five Hooks of Every Video

Great content creators create synergy between all five hooks:

### 1. Verbal Hook (What you say)

First three sentences. Do not rush through it. Main goal: open a curiosity loop. Include either a dream outcome or a pain point. A simple example: "Right now you are thinking, what are the five things?" That is the curiosity loop.

### 2. On-Screen Text Hook

Must correlate with but NOT duplicate what you said. The human mind can process a lot in three to five seconds. By making three different hooks in one, you theoretically get 15 seconds of brain processing in the first five seconds. The biggest mistake: putting the same text as your verbal hook.

### 3. Visual Hook

Something simple that is different. A hood pulled up. A 0.5x camera angle. All you are trying to do is not look like a generic talking head. Often a visual hook is just making sure you look like you belong on the platform.

### 4. Format Hook

Talking head, green screen, meta glasses, car recording. Inventing formats creates a hook rate by itself if people have never seen it before. A novel reaction video format was automatically high hook rate because nobody had seen it.

### 5. Caption Hook

The small text at the bottom. "Follow for more content strategy" - not a CTA, just a passive follow prompt. Can also serve as a second on-screen text hook.

### Context Coverage

Advanced hook technique: your on-screen text and verbal hook should cover as much of the video's content as possible, but from different angles.

Example: If your video has three garden tips (covered by on-screen text: "3 tips to garden better") AND a mathematical profit breakdown (covered by verbal hook: "the math behind 90% profit margins"), you have covered 66% of the video in the first 5 seconds. This creates a "curiosity bridge" - viewers are waiting for BOTH promises, driving retention through the middle of the video.

Most creators get one or the other. A great hook also drives watch time.

## Platform Differences

### TikTok vs. Instagram vs. YouTube

**YouTube**:

- Higher purchasing credibility metric than IG or TikTok. People are more likely to buy from creators on YouTube.
- "Content minutes" concept: more time spent with you equals more trust.
- Views went up in 2025 because fewer creators are entering. Higher bar to entry equals a stronger business moat.
- Revolves around packaging (thumbnails and titles). The video itself matters less compared to those.
- Weak for discovery. Reaching new people on YouTube is difficult.

**Instagram/TikTok**:

- Discovery platforms. Infinitely better for reaching new people.
- Short form develops raw skill set: better hooks, tighter communication.
- Still drives revenue, especially in the early stages of a creator business.
- Instagram: strong DM and CTA platform with real business impact.
- TikTok: favors volume, more forgiving of quality fluctuations.

**The verdict**: Start with short form. Build a following. Funnel to long form through stories, posts, and automation tools.

### Instagram-Specific Notes

- Never post 3x/day on Instagram. That is TikTok advice that does not apply.
- Use the Edits app for posting - it is getting boosted reach as a startup.
- Trial reels strategy is powerful on IG.
- Follower-first platform: your content goes to followers before non-followers, making follower quality critical.
- Stories are a conversion weapon: sell directly 50% of the time, add pure value the other 50%.

### TikTok-Specific Notes

- Favors volume. 2-3x/day is viable and often recommended.
- The 10-minute video format is counter-positioned and can work.
- TikTok Studio has deeper analytics than the main app.
- Lives convert at very high rates - your top 1% of followers show up.
- Start a niche-only second TikTok for doom-scrolling research.

## The AI Content Iceberg

A prediction for the AI era of content: creators will either be above the iceberg of AI-generated content or below it. Those below - with weak quality and skill sets - will be suppressed. Those above will be amplified.

The reasoning: AI will create an infinite supply of mediocre content. This increases demand for genuinely great content while flooding the zone with bad content. Bad content gets suppressed. If you can crack the threshold where AI quality stops, your content goes to the top.

The future is AI-assisted testing and distribution, with human creativity above the waterline.

## Eight Psychological Triggers for Content

From the book "Cashvertising," applied to content with examples:

1. **Enjoyment of life / life extension**: "Eat these three carrots to have more healthy joints in your thirties"
2. **Enjoyment of food/beverages**: "This recipe to have the best pizza you've ever had"
3. **Freedom from fear, pain, or danger**: "Carry this vegetable to avoid getting robbed at night"
4. **Sexual companionship**: "Eat this fruit and your downstairs juices will taste better"
5. **Comfortable living conditions**: "This fruit helps you profit more income to get out of debt faster"
6. **Superior/winning/keeping up with the Joneses**: "Make this recipe when friends come over to signal you are high status"
7. **Care and protection of loved ones**: "Make this natural smoothie to help your old mom avoid cancer"
8. **Social approval**: "Make this recipe at the friend dinner so everyone sees you as the backbone of the friend group"

Weaving these triggers into content and topic selection makes a measurable difference in performance.

## The Sweetening Trick

A specific production technique that increases retention 15-20%:

1. Edit in CapCut desktop
2. Raise speed to 1.05x (1.1x is too fast and becomes annoying)
3. Go to Audio - Voice Changer - scroll to "Pitch Up"
4. These two adjustments "sweeten" your audio the same way classic hit recordings were pitched up with tape machines to hit what some call the "love frequency"

Speeding music or tones up and down is scientifically proven to affect emotional response. Running this technique across clients averaged roughly 20% more views monthly.

## Customizing Your Algorithm

On Instagram Reels tab: bottom right button - top right settings (two lines and a heart). This shows what IG thinks you are interested in. You can:

- Add interests you actually want (AI, content creation, business)
- Remove interests that trigger dopamine traps
- Use "what you want to see less of" as the key feature

The average person spends five hours on social media a day. Curating that experience creates a massive compounding advantage over time.

## Tactical Playbook

### Daily Routine

1. **Morning pages** (from "The Artist's Way"): Write 3 pages of whatever is on your mind. Do not lift the pen. This clears mental noise so creative ideas surface.
2. **Post trial reels**: 10-20 B-roll one-liners
3. **Check yesterday's data**: Which trial reel had lowest skip rate? Which main video had best followers-per-view?
4. **Reply to every comment with a question**: Turn 1 comment into 3
5. **Farm 2-3 hater comments**: Use the two phrases mentioned above

### Weekly Process

1. **Think stadium, not machine** - ask "would this get a reaction from 100,000 people?"
2. **Analyze TAM before posting** - is this topic broad enough to reach your growth goals?
3. **Use trial reels for testing** - 10 hook variations, measure skip rate only
4. **Maintain algorithm momentum** - do not take long breaks, your baseline will crash
5. **Use Shikaki design** - place visual cues over like/save/share buttons
6. **Post from Edits app** on Instagram for boosted reach
7. **Run story engagement plays** - emoji responses, polls, questions
8. **Doom scroll your niche TikTok** - find formats, vocabulary, and comment opportunities

### Escaping 300-View Jail

1. Audit your follower quality - if followers are bought or from the wrong niche, consider starting fresh
2. Go to Reddit, find top-of-month conversations in your niche
3. Use AI to identify the two arguing sides and their vocabulary
4. Make a video positioned in the middle, taking a side at the end
5. Ensure your hook targets your ICP in the first 3 seconds
6. Use all 5 hooks (verbal, on-screen text, visual, format, caption)
7. Reply to every comment to boost engagement signals

### Content Time Allocation

If you have one hour:

- 40% ideation (scraping, research, Reddit, data analysis)
- 20% growth tactics (trial reels, comment farming, ManyChat)
- 15% strategy (top/middle/bottom funnel balance)
- 10% hooks
- 5% scripting
- 5% editing
- 5% copywriting practice

## Key Principles (Condensed)

- 99% of algorithm tips are noise. The topic of the video is the number 1 factor.
- Consistency matters for feedback loop speed, not for "training the algorithm."
- You post to gather data to innovate and improve. Not to satisfy a posting schedule.
- Algorithms find information people did not know they needed - information content will not die.
- Going viral is often bad for your account if it brings the wrong followers.
- The algorithm is a crowd to be moved, not a machine to be gamed.
- Fixing any one of three problems - no data, no psychology, no strategic connection - puts you in the top 1% of content.
- Design shapes behavior. Use Shikaki principles in your editing choices.
- Stories are algorithmic. Treat them accordingly.
- The human mind processes a lot in three to five seconds. Use all five hooks together.
