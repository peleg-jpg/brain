# How Posting Frequency Affects Per-Video Views - and Why That Metric Is the Wrong One to Watch

## Core Insight

When a creator increases posting frequency, individual video view counts will typically drop. This is not a signal of failure - it is a predictable consequence of view distribution. Every viewer has a finite attention budget per day: a limited number of videos they will scroll through before leaving the platform. When a creator publishes one video per day, all of their algorithmic surface area concentrates into that single piece of content. When they publish two, that same audience demand gets split across both videos, reducing per-video numbers.

The critical reframe is that per-video views are the wrong unit of measurement. The correct metric is total views week-over-week. Increasing output expands a creator's total surface area in the feed, reaching more people across more viewing sessions and generating more aggregate reach - even if no single video performs as well as before.

## Key Tactics

- Track week-over-week total views rather than individual video performance when evaluating a posting frequency change.
- Expect a temporary drop in per-video views when scaling from one to two posts per day - treat it as a structural shift, not a downward trend.
- Only increase posting frequency once a core content skill has been sufficiently developed; volume amplifies what is already working, it does not fix what is broken.
- Use increased posting as a deliberate growth lever when preparing to reach the next tier of audience size, not as a default strategy.
- Set realistic expectations before scaling up - psychological preparedness prevents creators from reverting prematurely based on misleading short-term signals.

## Why It Works

The underlying mechanism is supply and demand at the feed level. A platform's algorithm distributes content to each user based on their preferences and limited daily scroll capacity. More content from the same creator increases the total probability of appearing in that capacity across multiple sessions, raising aggregate reach. Follower conversion improves not because any single video is stronger, but because more people encounter the creator across more moments - compounding discovery opportunities over time.
