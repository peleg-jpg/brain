# Sends-Per-View and the Ideal Group Profile: Designing Content for Group Chats

## Core Insight

The source video presents a metric-driven argument for shifting how creators conceptualize their target audience. Rather than defining an ideal customer profile around an individual viewer's demographics and pain points, the more powerful frame is an ideal group profile - the specific social unit (a friend group, a Slack channel, a family group chat) that would collectively share and discuss a piece of content. Sends-per-view, described as a top-tier platform metric, is the quantitative signal this frame optimizes for.

The distinction matters because content designed to resonate with an individual is shared when that individual thinks "I need this," while content designed to resonate with a group dynamic is shared when someone thinks "this is for us." The second trigger fires more reliably and carries social validation with it - the receiver sees the content in the context of a trusted relationship, which dramatically increases follow and engagement probability.

## Key Tactics

- **Shift from individual ICP (ideal customer profile) to group IGP (ideal group profile)**: Write out the name of the specific group chat or Slack channel that would share your content, what those people talk about, and what would make one of them send the video to the group.
- **Use communal language constructions instead of individual-address phrasing**: Framing content around shared experience ("the team," "you and your training partner," "your group chat") rather than solitary experience ("I did this," "your personal results") makes sharing feel natural and socially appropriate.
- **Visualize the specific people in the target group while recording**: Keeping a concrete mental image of two or three real social groups - including what makes them laugh, what would blow their minds, or what would mildly provoke them - produces more emotionally calibrated content than writing to an abstract persona.
- **Design each video to trigger one of three social sharing emotions**: Content gets shared in group chats when it produces surprise/insight ("this will blow your mind"), productive friction ("this is going to start an argument"), or humor ("you have to see this"). Building toward one of these three outcomes intentionally raises sends-per-view.
- **Prompt the shift explicitly in video language**: The act of saying "you and your team need to think about this" within a video is itself a communal language cue that primes the viewer for group-context sharing.

## Why It Works

Sends (direct shares to other users or group chats) carry disproportionate algorithmic weight because they represent high-intent engagement - a viewer valued the content enough to actively route it to someone else's attention. Content that regularly generates sends gets surfaced to broader audiences through the social graph of the people who sent it, not just the creator's existing followers. Designing explicitly for the social sharing context rather than the individual viewing context taps a distribution channel that purely self-directed content misses entirely.
