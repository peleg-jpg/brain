# The Accordion Method: A Stage-Based Posting Frequency Model for TikTok Growth

## Core Insight

There is no universal correct posting frequency - the right cadence is a function of where a creator currently sits in their development arc. The principle taught is a three-stage model: beginners post less but invest more effort per video; developing creators aggressively expand posting frequency (the "accordion stretch") to gather data at scale; and creators who hit a plateau compress back down to fewer, higher-quality posts to break through to the next level.

The most counterintuitive element of the model is what happens when a developing creator jumps from one post per day to three: per-video view counts drop immediately, which feels like regression. The principle is that this feeling is a false signal. The relevant metric is total channel views per week or month, not per video - and that number grows during the high-frequency phase even when individual numbers look worse. Creators who do not understand this often panic and reduce frequency at precisely the wrong moment.

## Key Tactics

- Do not benchmark your growth against per-video view counts when posting at high frequency - track weekly or monthly channel totals instead.
- Apply a 70-30 split within whatever frequency level you are at: seventy percent proven formats, thirty percent wild-card experiments.
- Recognize the plateau signal - a long flat period after initial growth - as the specific trigger to compress frequency and increase quality per post.
- Use the high-frequency phase to collect systematic data: which topics, hooks, formats, and visual styles perform, and which consistently underperform.
- Avoid applying TikTok frequency advice to Instagram - the platforms operate on different algorithmic and audience expectation models; high-frequency posting rarely helps and often hurts on Instagram.

## Why It Works

High posting frequency during the development phase functions as an accelerated data collection mechanism. Each video is an experiment, and more experiments per unit of time means faster feedback loops. The algorithm also tends to reward channels that demonstrate consistent activity - not just individual video quality. During this phase, the creator is building three things simultaneously: on-camera skill through repetition, audience pattern data through observation of what resonates, and algorithmic credibility through consistency. The plateau that eventually emerges signals that the current format set has been fully explored, at which point quality becomes the differentiating variable again.
