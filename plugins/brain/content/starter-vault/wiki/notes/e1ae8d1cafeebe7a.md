# Why Educators Must Stay Active Practitioners - The 20% Rule

## Core Insight

There is a critical difference between someone who teaches a skill and someone who actively practices it. In fast-moving fields - whether content strategy, technology, or any craft subject to rapid change - instructors who have fully stepped away from doing the actual work quickly lose their ability to give useful day-to-day guidance. Their foundational principles may remain valuable, but the tactical, week-to-week advice they offer drifts out of sync with current reality.

The principle drawn from management thinking is that the best experts spend at least 20% of their time doing the ground-level work, not just managing or teaching. For anyone selling knowledge or coaching, this is a credibility and quality test: are they actively producing results in the domain they teach, or have they fully transitioned into selling the knowledge itself? The further they drift from active practice, the more their advice lags behind what actually works today.

## Key Tactics

- Before purchasing any coaching product or course, ask directly whether the instructor currently runs active client work or projects in the field they teach - not what they did historically.
- Look for educators who maintain a live practice alongside their teaching, so the tactical advice they give is continuously tested against current conditions.
- Treat foundational principles from any program as the durable value, and calibrate skepticism toward the tactical, platform-specific advice from people not actively in the field.
- Notice whether an educator can give real-time, week-to-week observations - if their updates are always generic or dated, it signals disconnection from active practice.
- Use the "20% ground-level" standard as a filter: the educator should be generating current results alongside teaching.

## Why It Works

Fast-moving fields change at a pace that punishes those who step back from active practice. Principles evolve slowly; tactics evolve weekly. An educator who is generating live results can observe what is actually working right now and feed that observation back into their teaching. One who only sells knowledge operates on increasingly outdated information. The asymmetry between slow-changing principles and fast-changing tactics is exactly why active practice is non-negotiable for high-quality, ongoing instruction.
