# The Three-Part Framework for Structuring Videos That Actually Grow a Business

## Core Insight

Most content advice focuses on surface-level structure - hooks, transitions, value delivery. The more durable framework operates at the level of concept design, not execution. Three principles must intersect for a video to truly perform: a balanced total addressable market (TAM), a unique and valuable angle, and a connection to the creator's business model.

The TAM principle is the most commonly misunderstood. Every video implicitly targets a specific audience through its framing - and most creators either go too broad (losing niche credibility) or too narrow (missing growth potential). The solution is an accordion approach: compress the concept enough to resonate with a core niche audience, then package it in a frame wide enough to attract casual and new viewers simultaneously. The goal is not a large TAM - it is a *balanced* one that serves all three audience layers at once without sacrificing any of them.

## Key Tactics

- Apply the CCN model when ideating: ask whether the video concept reaches the Core audience (existing followers), Casual audience (interested bystanders), and a New audience (people outside the niche) - all at once.
- Reframe niche-specific stories into universal curiosity hooks. A pilot flying to 22 golf courses in 22 days appeals to almost anyone, while still attracting aviation enthusiasts and business-minded viewers.
- Separate the unique angle from the value delivery. A contrarian or underexplored take earns attention; the actionable tips earn trust. Both are required - neither alone is sufficient.
- Avoid creating content that goes viral but has no business connection. Viral content that doesn't map back to a product, service, or offer actively harms channel alignment and monetization.
- Make the business connection implicit in the concept, not bolted on at the end. The video premise itself should make it natural that the creator offers something related.

## Why It Works

Audiences make snap decisions about relevance based on framing, not content quality. A video with a hyper-niche hook filters out potential new followers before they even watch; a video with an overly broad hook loses the trust of the core audience. The accordion method works because it exploits the gap between a concept's surface packaging (wide appeal) and its depth (niche specificity) - the same video can satisfy both types of viewers without compromise. Adding business alignment ensures that even well-performing videos contribute to a sustainable creator economy, not just vanity metrics.
