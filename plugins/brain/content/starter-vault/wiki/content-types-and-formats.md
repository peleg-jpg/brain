# Content Types and Formats

Two of the fastest ways to immediately double your views: better formats and a better personality.

## Core Principles

### Format as a Hook in Itself

One of the most important insights in content strategy: the format of your video IS a hook, independent of what you say. If people have never seen a particular format in your niche before, it creates automatic curiosity and a high hook rate - even before a single word is spoken.

The principle is clear: inventing formats is the single most important skill for a creative director or scaling creator. You can borrow formats from other niches, but eventually you must create formats that nobody in your space has seen. A reaction video where someone plays a clip then pauses it to add commentary will get a high hook rate in most niches simply because it's novel - the viewer has never seen that visual structure there before.

The framework goes further: you no longer even need to invent new knowledge. The knowledge already exists. What you need to invent is the format that delivers it in a way people haven't seen.

### Pattern Interruption Through Format

Pattern interruption is the number one skill in content AND in marketing in general. The Liquid Death case study is the clearest illustration: in 2018, the water bottle industry was dominated by Aquafina (13%), Dasani (9%), Smart Water (7%), Evian (2%), Fiji (2%). Liquid Death launched with wild branding and messaging that stood out from every existing player. By 2025, Liquid Death had captured 3% market share - making them the #4 water brand. Translating that to creator terms, 3% of a market equates to roughly a billion views a year.

The principle: look at what everyone else in your niche is doing, and do something different. If you have a niche full of people yapping in the car, go do a green screen. If your niche is full of green screen, go do a vlog. If everyone does talking head, do object lessons.

A concrete example: Jeff Magnuson in the fitness niche. While everyone does gym lifts and mirror selfies, Magnuson does object lessons at a desk. He's effectively counter-positioning the entire fitness format landscape.

### Import Formats From Other Niches

One of the most practical tactics available: create a second TikTok account, train it only on your niche, then use your personal TikTok to consume other niches. Look for formats that work elsewhere but haven't appeared in your niche yet.

When scrolling a niche-specific account, look for three things: unique opportunities to import formats from outside, niche vocabulary that signals what's resonating, and comment patterns that reveal audience hunger. Ask: "What am I seeing over here that is not on my niche's TikTok?"

If you're in the gardening niche, there's already plenty of green screen and vlog gardening. But if you scroll your personal feed and see a DJ do a whiteboard - which makes no sense in context - that's worth thinking about. Can you bring the whiteboard format into gardening?

Think beyond social media. Pull from television shows. Family Feud, ESPN, Modern Family, NCIS - is there anything that can become an Instagram video rather than a talking head or crazy edit? A creative director for a big podcast who starts doing a Family Feud format would stand out completely.

### Three Ways to Counter-Position

Three distinct methods to pattern interrupt and stand out:

**1. Format** - Do what nobody in your niche is doing visually. If everyone talks in the car, go green screen. If everyone does green screen, go vlog. If everyone does talking head, do object lessons.

**2. Niche Pocket** - Go up (luxury), down (beginner), or cross (add a flavor from another niche). Andrew Huberman added science to self-improvement. Shelby Stapp brought a distinct identity to the sales niche. These are cross-niche flavor additions that make a creator stand out in two domains at once.

**3. Branding/Messaging** - Change how you present yourself relative to the norm. A niche full of Miami gurus with expensive cars is immediately disrupted by someone doing the same content from a modest home in Omaha, Nebraska - whether or not that person is actually more trustworthy, it reads that way.

### Uniqueness is the #1 Attribute

Consider this thought experiment: would you rather be an expert, more unique, the best communicator, the wealthiest, or the hottest? The answer every time: unique.

The uncomfortable truth about social media is that people follow people who are unique, not people who are actually the best experts. A top Harvard research assistant who cannot communicate more uniquely than everyone else will not get followed. That's the reality of the platform. Being unique beats being right.

Five categories where uniqueness can be applied for follower growth:

1. **Unique value** - If you're repeating what already-famous creators said, that follower slot is already filled. They can just follow the original.
2. **Unique perspective** - Can you change their worldview? Blow their mind on how they think about something?
3. **Unique humor** - Most people aren't genuinely funny. If you are, and your humor is unique, it's powerful. But don't force it if it isn't natural.
4. **Empathy** - Relating to the viewer through shared experience. An amputee making content about life with one arm commands instant loyalty from a specific audience.
5. **Aspirational content** - The hardest category to convert from, because most people's lives are not uniquely aspirational. A generic day-in-the-life at a normal gym doesn't inspire follows.

Apply uniqueness to one of these five categories and the growth equation changes completely.

## Format Tier Ranking (S to D)

A comprehensive ranking of common formats by current performance:

### S Tier

**Reaction with Commentary** - You play a viral video, pause it, and add your expert commentary between sections. This is the format to be doing right now. You borrow the hook rate from the already-viral video (people recognize it), position yourself as an authority by commenting on it, and pull credibility from the original creator. You'll get strong leads and authority-building in one shot.

**Carousels** - Currently performing exceptionally well on Instagram. The critical distinction: they must be shareable. Shareability is the ONLY way carousels reach non-followers. If a carousel isn't built to be sent to a friend or shared on a story, it will only hit your existing audience.

**Notes App Green Screen** - Show a notes app behind you on green screen, with on-screen text up top as the hook. Bonus points if you can partially cover the text with your head to build curiosity about what's hidden. That combination of visual format and curiosity gap makes this S tier.

### A Tier

**Split Screen** - You on top, supporting visual on bottom (or vice versa). Works regardless of topic and is easy to produce once you get efficient at it. Doesn't require a special concept - the format carries a lot of the weight.

**Top Down** - Camera directly above looking down at something (hands, desk, objects). Requires a hook where you face the camera first, then transition to top-down. The transition itself creates visual interest.

**Object Lessons** - Using a physical object as a metaphor throughout your video. The critical mistake most people make: they show the object once at the start and then never reference it again. The object needs to progress alongside the storytelling. Don't just introduce an object, mention it once, and move on. Carry it through the whole video.

**Ranking/Interview Ranking** - Ranking things on screen or interview-style rankings. Solid and easy to scale, but requires a genuinely interesting concept to carry it.

**Triple Clone Video** - You appear as three versions of yourself, each representing a different perspective (e.g., $1M founder, $10M founder, $100M founder). Strong format but getting overplayed - use thoughtfully.

### B Tier

**Whiteboard** - Many creators love whiteboard content personally, but the data says it's B tier. It doesn't perform as strongly as the formats above it. Personal taste doesn't override metrics.

**Normal Clone Video** - Two versions of yourself (not three). Good for contrast but less impactful than the triple version.

### D Tier

**Day in the Life** - Nobody cares about your daily life unless you've accomplished something remarkable. If you haven't, this format won't work.

**Podcast Format** - Overdone and weak for growth, especially fake podcasts where you're sitting in front of a mic with nothing to show for it. If you have a real podcast, clip and repurpose it - but the format itself is not a growth driver.

**Skits** - Generally not worth attempting. The bar is extremely high and the failure rate is nearly total.

## Talking Head Videos

### The Visual Hook Problem

The default talking head format is the most common and therefore has the lowest built-in hook rate. If you're going to do talking head, you need a visual hook that differentiates you from every other person on camera talking.

The goal: don't look like a standard talking head video with a nice camera and a podcast mic. That's the default. Everything default gets scrolled past.

### The Death Row Visual Hook Question

A useful thought experiment to illustrate the importance of visual hooks: you're on death row and the judge says that if you can get a 1 million viewed talking head video, you get to walk free. What visual hook do you choose?

Options to consider:

- Classic podcast setup
- Infrared glasses
- Pen and paper
- A weapon
- Cleavage
- Rolex, chains, and money

The exercise forces you to think about what single visual element would maximize the hook rate of an otherwise plain talking head video. The answer reveals what your current setup is missing.

### What to Wear on Camera

Clothing strategy has three attributes worth optimizing:

**1. Pattern Interrupt** - Clothing should stop the scroll. Use contrast against your background. Strong contrast from your backdrop creates visual differentiation. Even holding a pen creates a "visual pacifier" - a small pattern interrupt that keeps the eye interested.

**2. Association** - What do you want people to mentally categorize you as? If you give tax advice, wear a suit. If you're a doctor or therapist, wear scrubs. Accessories from brands that attract specific demographics serve as identity signals.

**3. Conversation Starters** - Wear items that provoke comments. Inside joke shirts, sports jerseys (people will argue in the comments), politically charged hats, funny cultural references. A sports jersey might generate both support and opposition - that's the point. More comments, even disagreements, boost the video.

The math compounds: if your videos average 3,000 views and 15 comments, getting just 3-5 extra comments per video could move views from 3,000 to 5,000. Over a month, that compounds into nearly double the follower growth.

## B-Roll and Visual Strategy

### B-Roll as Visual Hook

B-roll serves as one of the five hook types. It gives the viewer something to process visually while the verbal hook delivers different information. This is the context coverage concept: the eyes process the visual while the ears process the verbal, covering more of the video's content in the first seconds.

### The B-Roll One-Liner Method (for Trial Reels)

A specific tactic for testing at scale:

1. Take 20 photos from your photo album
2. Animate them in VO3 (6-7 seconds each)
3. Go to Reddit, YouTube comments, podcast comments in your niche
4. Paste all that content into ChatGPT: "Give me 20 one-liners that match my niche"
5. Put each one-liner on a different B-roll video
6. Post all 20 as trial reels every day
7. Look for outliers - which one-liner got 10x baseline views?
8. Analyze: what was the buzzword? The emotion? The pain point?
9. Make a full video about that topic

The target is finding the hair of outliers. If your baseline is 500 views, the one-liner that hits 5,000 tells you what topic has demand. Build the full video from that signal.

## Carousel Strategy

### Two Types of Carousels

Two fundamentally different carousel strategies exist, and confusing them is a common mistake:

**1. Shareable Carousels (Growth)** - Designed to be sent to friends, shared in group chats, put on stories. These reach non-followers and drive growth. The content must embody who the viewer is - something they'd share to signal their identity. This is S-tier for growth.

**2. Life-Driven Carousels (Re-engagement)** - More personal, lifestyle-focused carousels that re-engage existing followers. Great for bottom-of-funnel content but not for audience expansion.

The critical insight: shareability is the ONLY mechanism by which carousels reach non-followers. If a carousel isn't shareable, it will only hit your existing audience.

## Production Quality vs Authenticity

### Minimal Setup Philosophy

The data strongly supports minimal production setups. An effective production setup can be:

- **Camera**: iPhone camera app, 0.5 on back cam
- **Light**: A budget ring light - nothing elaborate
- **Microphone**: Optional. Many successful creators don't use one.
- **Recording**: One cut. Maybe redo the hook a few times, but the body is one take.
- **Outline, not script**: Just keywords on a notes app. For a video about "how I read," the entire outline might be: "barbell approach, kill your vibes, read for retention, Spotify" with a few sub-notes.

Scripting destroys naturalness for most creators. A spoken outline produces more authentic delivery than reading lines. One-cut recording forces you to be clean and stops you from over-editing before you've even started.

### Editing Should Be Minimal

In CapCut, three things only:

1. Cut dead space and trim edges
2. Turn speed up to 1.1x
3. Add any images

Then export from CapCut and do final editing in the Edits app (for Instagram) or TikTok app (for TikTok). The Instagram CEO confirmed that the Edits app is currently giving further reach.

### Time Allocation

Editing and production should be about 5% of your total content creation time. A practical time allocation breakdown:

- 40% ideation
- 20% growth tactics
- 15% strategy (top/middle/bottom funnel balance)
- 10% hooks
- 5% scripting/storytelling
- 5% editing/production
- 5% copywriting skill development

When someone asks what's the most important part of a video, the answer is not the edit.

### Perfectionism is a Curse

Perfectionism is only a curse. Not a blessing and a curse. Only a curse.

Content is not filmmaking, where you make 5-10 projects and each has to be perfect. Content has feedback loops that change every 24 hours. The speed to feedback is what makes a great content creator - not the polish of any individual video.

The three attributes shared by the fastest-growing creators in terms of anti-perfectionism:

1. Not afraid to test things at scale without needing proof of concept first
2. Don't let bad recording days slow down the production feedback loop - ship it anyway
3. Let the natural tension between high-quality/low-quantity and low-quality/high-quantity phases exist

Ship a C+ piece of content. That's fine. Next week it'll be a B+.

### No AI Scripting

Using AI to write your scripts is a fast path to content that sounds like AI. AI should be used for ICP research, A/B testing hooks, cross-posting strategy, TAM analysis, and building GPTs as lead magnets. Never for writing the words you say on camera.

## Format Testing and Iteration

### The 70/30 Split

The foundational rule for balancing proven content with experimentation:

70% should be content you feel confident will hit. 30% should be wild card videos where you're stepping outside your box and testing new formats.

### Upside Thinking (from Fantasy Football)

Content has a "hair of luck" involved, similar to fantasy football or Settlers of Catan. You can't treat it like a job where effort directly equals output. This requires "upside thinking."

In fantasy football, the first five rounds you draft for value (safe picks). After round five, you draft for upside - rookies who might become superstars or might bust completely. Content works the same way.

You need your base formats, base hooks, and base concepts. But you also need to be testing things at an uncomfortable rate - formats and ideas that have real upside and real risk of failing.

A 25-year-old baking creator who crushes with recipe content should keep 70-80% recipes. The other 20-30% should be pranking her boyfriend with terrible food, split screen clone talking to herself, green screen (nobody in baking does this). She needs to become deeply okay with the fact that those experiments will sometimes fail completely.

### Six Attempts Per Test

Don't judge a new format on one video. Give every experiment six attempts before deciding if it works. And don't compare views between them emotionally - look at trend, not individual data points.

### The Innovation Compression Cycle

When you hit a plateau (often around 50K followers), don't change niches. Compress from 3 videos/day to 1 really good video/day. This is the time to:

- Start a series (e.g., "30 days of chopping down a tree")
- Get a new format
- Hire an editor
- Go higher production
- Experiment with green screen

The plateau usually requires breaking through with quality and innovation, not just more volume. Volume got you here. Quality and format innovation get you through.

### Find Your Signature Formats

After the volume phase (see [[posting-frequency]]), you should have enough data to identify your signature formats and topics. What are your green screens hitting? Your reaction videos? The data from volume gives you the answer. Stage three is identifying those signature formats and doubling down.

## Video Chapters Method (Data-Driven Content)

### Break Videos Into Chapters

Every video is composed of "chapters" - distinct sections that each serve a different purpose. By breaking your best (and worst) videos into chapters, you can identify which specific chapter types drive views.

Example: scene one was a historical story. Scene two was three steps. Scene three was a controversial take. If that's your most viral video, you need to know which chapter was responsible. Was it the historical story? The step-by-step framework? The controversial take? This is a game of mix-and-match.

### The Card Deck Analogy (Full Method)

Take three videos with different view counts (e.g., 6K, 2K, 7K). Break each into its "ingredients" (chapters):

- Video 1 (6K): Hook with hot take - historical story - statistic - three action steps
- Video 2 (2K): [whatever chapters it had]
- Video 3 (7K): Hook with quick how-to - historical story - mathematical breakdown - acronym framework

**Step 1**: Remove the flop. The 2K video didn't work. Move it completely off the board.

**Step 2**: Find commonalities between the two winners. Both had historical stories - that's a confirmed winning chapter type.

**Step 3**: Go into the comments to see what people specifically responded to. If nobody commented about the statistic but multiple people asked about one specific action step, that action step is your signal.

**Step 4**: Combine winning chapters. New video = hook (whichever had better hook rate) + historical story (confirmed winner) + single deep-dive on the most-requested sub-topic + mathematical breakdown (numbers worked, keep testing).

This is how a 6K viewed video becomes a 77K video. Not luck. Data.

## Communication Frameworks

### Explain - Illustrate - Apply

The core communication framework, adapted from public speaking:

**Explain** - Simply, uniquely, with personality. Fifth grade reading level.
**Illustrate** - Metaphors, examples, statistics, quotes, books, listicles, step-by-step processes.
**Apply** - Tactical, unique advice. Not "do better push-ups" but "do a 10 by 3 where you roll your elbow in."

### Communal Language for Sends

The key to getting shares: use language that references groups, not individuals.

Instead of "I went to this hotel," say "me and the boys honeymoon getaway." That's a phrase that gets shared to a spouse. The person receiving it thinks: this is for us.

You need an Ideal Group Profile (IGP) - not just an Ideal Customer Profile (ICP). Visualize the specific group chat you want your video shared in. Know the name of the group, what they talk about, what they share. Write it on paper and put it behind your camera every time you record.

Three emotional levers for group sharing:

1. Blow their mind (educational shock)
2. Piss them off (provocative takes)
3. Make them laugh (shared humor)

### FDR's Communication Secret

Studying all 31 of FDR's fireside radio chats reveals the key separator: pain point awareness. FDR knew exactly what Americans feared at each specific moment - banking crisis, farm sustainability, unemployment - and spoke directly to those fears. He described the people of America as "intelligent but anxious," which is an extremely rare combo that required a specific communication approach.

Questions to ask about your audience:

1. What are their pain points?
2. Can you describe them in 1-3 emotions? (e.g., "intelligent but anxious," "overworked but underpaid," "young but playful")
3. Can you speak to that exact emotional convergence?

## Inventing New Formats

### The Creative Director's Mandate

For anyone working as a creative director or trying to scale their own brand past 100K: format invention is the job. Not content production - format invention.

Where to find format inspiration:

- Television shows (Family Feud, ESPN, Modern Family, NCIS)
- Other niches on TikTok
- Real-world experiences outside any screen

At minimum 30% of formats should be new experiments - wild card ideas, the most unexpected things possible.

### The Contextualization Framework for Formats

The same framework used for ideation (see [[content-market-fit]]) applies to formats:

1. Observe a format in another niche or medium
2. Identify the underlying principle that makes it work
3. Find the relationship to your niche's underlying principles
4. Create a version for your niche

Example: A ChatGPT draft video (where someone drafts WNBA players) could become a "draft against ChatGPT on the top 5 clothing companies with the best brand positioning" - same format, different niche application. Then iterate further: draft by specific features (logo only, color only, typography only). Each layer is a new video from the same creative bridge.

## The Scaling Content Engine

### Transcending to the Next Level (100K+ to millions)

For creators at 100-300K wanting to break through to millions, a multi-layered system modeled on the highest-growth creators works like this:

**Layer 1**: Massive raw footage generation - 10+ hours per week from podcasts, business meetings, vlogs, videographer following you around

**Layer 2**: Clipping with playbooks - 4-5 editors from the Philippines with documented SOPs. Each clip gets 10 different hooks. 10 hours of raw content becomes ~4,000 pieces of content for ~$10,000.

**Layer 3**: Multi-channel distribution - Burner TikTok accounts, trial reels on Instagram, second Instagram account. Winners from testing get moved to the main profile.

**Layer 4**: Funnel-specific editors - One editor clips for top-of-funnel (sends to YouTube/Instagram), one for middle-of-funnel (sends to email sequences), one for bottom-of-funnel (sends to landing pages).

**Layer 5**: Email marketing via Substack - All traffic funneled to a 5-day educational email course (informed by which viral videos performed best).

**Layer 6**: Monthly podcast outreach - One strategic podcast appearance per month for credibility and long-form content.

This is how creators move from 300K to 1.5M. Not by making better individual videos, but by building an engine.

## Tactical Playbook

1. Rank your current formats against the S-D tier list - are you using any S tier formats?
2. Import one format from a completely different niche into yours
3. Test new formats with the 70/30 rule - 70% proven formats, 30% wild experiments
4. Give each new format 6 attempts before judging
5. Break your best videos into "chapters" - find which chapter types drive retention
6. Use the Card Deck method: find commonalities between winners, read comments, combine winning chapters
7. Use the object lesson format but carry the object through the entire video (don't just show it once)
8. Keep production minimal - one cut, outline not script, 1.1x speed in CapCut
9. Think in communal language - "me and the boys" not "I did this"
10. Define your Ideal Group Profile - know the exact group chat you want this shared in
11. Wear clothes that create pattern interrupt (contrast), association (identity signal), or conversation (comments)
12. For carousels: shareable ones for growth (must be send-worthy), life-driven for re-engagement
13. Try the B-roll one-liner method with 20 trial reels per day to find winning topics
14. Invent at least one new format - pull from TV shows, other niches, or real life
15. Apply uniqueness to at least one of: value, perspective, humor, empathy, aspiration
16. At plateau, compress from volume to one high-quality video per day with new formats
17. Don't over-edit - spend 5% of total time on editing/production
18. Never use AI for scripting - use outlines only for natural delivery

## Key Principles Summary

- Two fastest ways to double views: better formats and better personality
- Format itself is a hook - novelty in your niche creates automatic curiosity
- Pattern interruption is the number one skill in content and in marketing generally
- Import formats from TV shows, other niches, unrelated contexts
- Uniqueness beats expertise on every social platform
- S tier now: reaction with commentary, notes app green screen, shareable carousels
- Object lessons must carry through the whole video, not just the opening
- Minimal production setup (iPhone, one cut, outline) outperforms over-produced content
- Perfectionism slows feedback loops and kills growth - ship C+ now, iterate to B+
- 70/30 split: proven formats vs. experiments at all times
- Six attempts minimum before judging a new format
- At plateau: compress to 1 video/day, innovate formats, then scale back up
- Communal language ("me and the boys") drives shares; individual language ("I did this") doesn't
- Never AI-script your words on camera
