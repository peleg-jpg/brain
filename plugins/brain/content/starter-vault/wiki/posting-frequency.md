# Posting Frequency

How often should you post? This is more complicated than most advice suggests. Anyone who just says "post more" is wrong - as is anyone who says "always post as much as possible." Blanket statements on frequency should be dismissed entirely.

## Core Principles

### It Depends on Where You Are

There is no single right answer to posting frequency. Anyone giving a blanket statement is wrong, and anyone giving non-nuanced advice should not be listened to about anything.

There is not an easy answer to this. It depends on where you're at. Anybody who says don't post daily is wrong. Anybody who says always post as much as possible is also wrong. It depends on where you're at in your creator journey.

Everybody just keeps saying post more content, more volume, 20 times a day. It's just not true anymore. Because everyone is at a different skill set, a different place in their journey, and that advice is a very blanket statement.

If someone is giving specific non-nuanced advice, don't listen to them about anything. They're just saying that for the algorithm.

### As Frequent as You Can Without Losing Quality

This is the underlying principle across all frequency guidance. Quality must come first, then you stretch volume as far as quality allows.

### The Skill Must Come Before the Volume

The most common mistake: posting 3 times a day without understanding hooks, pacing, ideation, or on-screen text. This is like swinging a baseball bat without knowing if you're right-handed or left-handed.

There's no point in posting a ton of content if you don't have the skill set. It's like swinging a baseball bat without knowing if you're right or left-handed. You're not going to get any better. There's no point hitting off a tee if you don't even know how to hold the bat.

If you are not able to get a thousand views per video consistently, you don't have the skill set of short-form yet. What do you do? You need to invest in consulting, read books, watch YouTube videos and podcasts.

Real-world example: one practitioner posted close to 500 pieces of content across client accounts before ever posting once on a personal channel. The first personal video blew up because there were 500 iterations of practice behind it.

Earlier in that same journey, there were 20-30 pieces on a personal account getting a few hundred views. The skills weren't there yet. Stepping away, getting better, and coming back prepared made all the difference.

## The Accordion Method

This framework, attributed to Caleb Ralston (Alex Hormozi's creative director, also Gary Vaynerchuk's creative director), is the primary model for thinking about posting frequency across an entire creator journey.

The analogy: your time and effort are like an accordion. Sometimes you compress (fewer videos, higher effort per video). Sometimes you stretch (more videos, distributed effort). The key is knowing when to do which.

### Phase 1: Compress - Learn the Skill (1x/day or less)

If you're a beginner who hasn't invested time in learning the actual skill of content creation, you need to post LESS and put MORE effort into making each video better.

If you are a beginner - if you do not spend any time trying to learn the skill of content - you need to post less and have more effort going into better videos.

Specific recommendation: 3-5 times per week. Use the remaining time to learn.

Three to five times a week to rip the bandaid off, get used to it, and spend one hour learning.

What to learn during this phase: copywriting basics, hooks, on-screen text, ideation, topic selection, camera basics, captions. It takes about 100 intentional iterations to acquire the skill set.

The goal of this phase: consistently hit 1,000 views per video, 3-4 times per week. Once you can do that, you've earned the skill set and can move to the next phase.

Another way to frame this phase: you're posting one video per day (or less), with the goal of getting 1% better each day. Maximum effort compressed into minimum output.

Posting one time per week while learning your skill set is appropriate for the zero to 1,000 to 5,000 views-per-video range.

### Phase 2: Stretch - Volume (2-3x/day)

Once you understand content and can make quality videos quickly - usually around 25-45 intentional videos in - it's time to stretch the accordion and go volume.

At some point in that journey you're going to reach a point where you understand content a little bit. It's probably going to be around the 25 to 45 intentional videos. You're going to start realizing how hooks work. Now you need to stretch the accordion.

You stretch the accordion now and you need to start posting three times a day.

**The panic warning** - This is critical because every single person who goes through this phase freaks out during it:

Here is the problem. Please listen. Because everyone who has done this freaks out in this moment. Whenever you post three times a day, your views are going to get diluted into three videos. So if you're usually getting 5,000 views a day, that's going to break up into three videos. Might be like 2,000, 2,000, 2,000.

The critical insight: per-video views go down, but your CHANNEL is growing. Week-over-week and month-over-month total views go up.

If you go into your week-over-week or month-over-month of views, your channel is growing and that is what matters.

If you're worried about the aesthetic of numbers on your profile because you're not getting 10,000 views anymore - get over it.

During this volume phase, collect data obsessively:

In this journey you should be collecting so much data. What videos work, what videos don't work, what on-screen text works, what formats work for you.

Use the 70/30 split during this phase: 70% proven content, 30% wild card experiments with new formats and topics. Run this for 90 days minimum.

Three times a day is the minimum for 90 days during this phase.

**Volume on TikTok, not Instagram**: This advice is specifically for TikTok. TikTok is a discovery-first platform - your content goes to new people first, not your followers. On Instagram, posting 3x/day causes "follower fatigue" because content goes to your followers first.

This is different for Instagram and TikTok. Specifically for TikTok. This advice doesn't really apply to Instagram. You should never be posting three times a day on Instagram. Rarely, rarely, rarely.

**Important**: You don't have to put all volume on your main page. Put volume on trial reels and TikTok burner accounts, then move winners to your main page.

You don't have to put it on your main page. Put it on trials, move the winner over. On volume, two to three times a day. And then also put on TikTok. You can go volume on TikTok.

### Phase 3: Compress Again - Quality Plateau Break (1x/day, high effort)

At some point during the volume phase, you'll hit a plateau. Views stagnate. Growth slows. This often happens around 50,000 followers, though it depends on the niche.

When this happens, do NOT change niches. Do NOT pivot randomly. Instead, compress the accordion back down.

Whenever you hit your plateau, you don't want to pivot, you don't want to change niches. What you want to do now is compress your three videos a day into one really good video a day.

This is the innovation phase:

Go back and find all of your great stuff. Maybe you now do a series. Or you get a new format. You hire an editor because your business is probably there. Now you start going a little bit more green screen, you go higher production. Because you need to break through the plateau and the way you do that usually is through quality.

What to do in this phase:

- Go back to your data and find all your winning content
- Start a series (e.g., "Day 1 of trying to chop down a tree")
- Invent a new format
- Hire an editor
- Go higher production (green screen, better visuals)
- Invest in a camera upgrade
- One video per day, maximum effort

Compress it back down. Go back to one video and raise the quality. Maybe it's time to invest in a camera. Maybe you invent a couple of new formats.

You will take a dip in views during this phase. That's expected. You're exploring, testing, and reinventing.

### Phase 4: Scale with Systems (2x/day + testing)

After breaking through the plateau with quality, it's time to scale again - but this time with systems and a team.

Build the systems. Get an editor. That's going to speed things up. Learn how to build SOPs and start scaling. You can now do two times a day. Maybe a carousel in the morning, a signature format at night. And then you should be maxing out your testing - trial reels 10 times a day on TikTok.

At this point you should be at around 50,000 followers and already monetizing. An editor and strategist can be hired overseas for $2-3K/month.

### Phase 5: Long Form

Only after mastering short form do you add YouTube or podcast.

Add YouTube or podcast after mastering short form.

## The 5 Stages (Detailed Breakdown)

The most comprehensive frequency framework:

**Stage 1 - Beginner (3-5x/week)**

- Learn the skill. Study copywriting. Consume YouTube education.
- Get 1% better every video.
- ~100 iterations to acquire the skill set.
- Don't worry about growth tactics, hashtags, posting times, or any "bells and whistles."

**Stage 2 - Skill Acquired (2-3x/day)**

- Volume time. Stretch the accordion.
- Put volume on trial reels and TikTok burner accounts, move winners to main.
- 90-day minimum run.
- Collect data: what works, what doesn't, what formats hit, what on-screen text works.
- Use the 70/30 split (proven content / wild cards).

**Stage 3 - Signature Found (1x/day, high quality)**

- Find your signature formats and topics from the data.
- Channel all effort into 1x/day proven content.
- This is where you move from "quantity leads to quality" to "quality at focused volume."

**Stage 4 - Scale (2x/day with systems)**

- Hire an editor. Build SOPs. Start scaling.
- Carousel in the morning, signature format at night.
- Max out trial reels (10x/day on TikTok).
- Should be around 50K followers and monetizing.

**Stage 5 - Long Form**

- Add YouTube or podcast.
- Only after short form is fully cracked and systematized.

## The MVC-to-Content-Market-Fit Journey

A startup-inspired framework (detailed in [[content-market-fit]]) directly shapes posting frequency:

**Sprint to MVC (Minimal Viable Content)**: Find your easiest possible way to produce content. For most creators, that's turning on the camera and talking. No fancy production.

**Sprint to Content Market Fit**: Post MVC at volume with different angles until you find what consistently gets 3-5K+ views within your brand pillars.

**Compress after finding fit**: Use the accordion method to focus only on your winners.

The flow between platforms:

1. **TikTok** (discovery platform) - Test at volume. MVC. Find themes that work.
2. **Instagram** (follower-centric) - Move polished winners from TikTok. Don't fatigue followers.
3. **YouTube** (long form) - Take all-time best performers and expand into long form. Proven concepts only.

You test on TikTok. You move over your polished stuff to Instagram because of the follower fatigue. Instagram is follower-centric. TikTok is for-you-centric.

### Follower Fatigue Warning

On Instagram specifically, posting too much low-quality content causes "follower fatigue" - your followers see bad content, stop engaging, which tells the algorithm to suppress your reach. This creates a downward spiral.

On Instagram, if you post 15 times, it's going to your viewers. They will get follower fatigue - they see your video, and if it's a bad piece of content, they don't even give you a chance. They just scroll past it.

This is why the TikTok-first testing strategy exists. TikTok doesn't dish content to followers first, so you can post volume without follower fatigue risk.

### Algorithm Momentum

There's a real (but often overstated) effect of posting consistently. When you stop posting, other creators fill your "slot" in your followers' feeds:

Stopping TikTok posts for a month caused a baseline view average to drop from 50,000 down to about 7,000. Because other creators moved into those viewers' top favorite slots during that month.

But the myth that consistency "trains the algorithm" should be debunked:

Most people think consistency matters because it trains the algorithm. If you post every single day at nine o'clock, it'll tell the algorithm that this person is doing so well. That's not true.

Going 8 days without posting and having the next video still perform at baseline (25-30K views) proves the point. Consistency matters for improving through feedback loops and maintaining your "slot" in followers' feeds - not for algorithmic rewards.

## Quality vs Quantity

### Quality First, Always

The fundamental principle: there is no point posting at volume if you haven't learned the skill. You're just creating noise.

There's no point in posting a ton of content if you don't have the skill set. It's like swinging a baseball bat without knowing if you're right or left-handed.

But once the skill is acquired, quantity becomes the path to finding what works:

Quantity will lead to quality. But if you don't even know what you should be doing, then quantity is just going to lead to trash.

### The Iterate-Don't-Just-Post Distinction

Posting and iterating are different things. Most creators spray and pray. Iterating means: post, analyze the data (topic, format, comments), innovate, post again with adjustments.

Most people just spray and pray. It's not gonna work. You have to analyze your data and move up and up and up until you eventually crack the code.

So many creators aren't iterating. Iterations and fine-tuning and improvement and testing are what lead to that one million-viewed video.

### The Cross-Posting Return

Cross-posting to additional platforms gives nearly 1:1 returns:

Every time volume moved up or cross-posting happened to multiple channels, there was about a 1:0.8 ratio of more times posted to dollars out. Posting five times and making five dollars, then posting ten times and making nine dollars - that is a phenomenal return. There's no decline there.

If you're serious about building a personal brand and aren't cross-posting to LinkedIn, X, and Threads, you're missing out. Drop your script into ChatGPT, say "turn this into a LinkedIn/Twitter post," and post. Can be delegated for $50/week.

### The Accordion and Content Market Fit Cycle

The full cycle in one sentence: Compress effort into learning (few posts, high effort per post) - stretch into volume once skilled (many posts, distributed effort) - collect data during volume - compress back into quality when plateaued (few posts, high effort, new formats) - stretch again with a team.

This cycle repeats throughout your entire creator career. You never "arrive" at a final frequency.

## The Healthy Relationship Principle

### The Most Important Thing a Creator Can Have

A quiz listing 11 attributes: hooks, storytelling, personality, data analysis, healthy relationship with content, editing, choosing ideas, building funnels, communication, branding, counter-positioning. Only one correct answer:

The only correct answer is having a healthy relationship with content to post every single day. This is the combination of joy plus obsession. If you don't have this, you're going to quit posting. And great storytelling, great hooks, everything else on this list does not matter if you don't post every single day.

This is the meta-skill that enables everything else. Without it, you quit, and all other skills become irrelevant.

### Think in Decades, Not Days

We do not think in days, we think in decades. The goal is posting content for 10 to 20 years, not just a few months.

You have to see your creator journey in decades rather than days. If you get tired and want to remix some posts, if you get burnt out, if you want to swap niches - the most important thing you can do is keep a healthy relationship with content.

### Roger Federer Analogy

From Federer's autobiography "Mastering the Long Game of Tennis," a key insight about balance:

Roger Federer is the best tennis player of all time. He said he practices the self-discipline of balance. When he's not feeling it, sometimes he just goes to the gym and talks to his trainer. He forces himself whenever he loses to cry, go eat with the family. Forces himself even whenever he wants to train - he'll go on a vacation.

The parallel: when you're burnt out, take a lighter day - remix posts, go simpler. When you're fired up, don't go so hard that you burn out next week. Play the long game.

The most important thing you can do is keep a healthy relationship with content. That's not sexy, that's not some cool tactic, it's not some cool hook - but it's the most important thing you can do to stay in the game.

### Don't Change Niches

One of the most destructive things a creator can do is change niches when things get hard. You lose all compound growth.

If you get tired or want to swap niches, you can't - because you lose the compound. It's like being number one in tennis and deciding to go play basketball. Terrible idea.

If you must pivot, stay in the "family tree." Fitness can move to nutrition. But fitness cannot randomly jump to space engineering.

Stay in the family tree. If you're from fitness, you can go into nutrition. But if you're in fitness, you can't randomly be like, I'm going to go talk about space engineering.

### The Sauna Endurance Analogy

A sauna practice as extended metaphor for business and content endurance, identifying five types of people:

**Type 1 - Clothes on, phone in hand (5 minutes)**: Treating the sauna/business as a pitstop. Shiny object syndrome. In and out in minutes.

**Type 2 - No clothes, still has phone (15 minutes)**: Better, but signals they won't last long. Makes it 1-2 years in business before quitting when it gets hard. "Didn't know Mount Everest was going to be cold."

**Type 3 - Towel only, but starts to spaz or brings ice chips**: Mentally committed but makes terrible emotional decisions during hard times. Buys the Lamborghini as a pacifier for identity because of deep insecurity.

**Type 4 - No tech, just a journal and towel**: Prepared for war. Works through hardship. The only path is through.

**Type 5 - Sweats on with an echo bike, ankles taped**: Has made suffering part of their identity. Walks in knowing they'll outlast everyone else.

What would it look like to make suffering and not quitting part of your identity? You have a silent chip on your shoulder. You just had the worst month in business you've ever had and you don't give a flying concern. It's part of who you are.

Personal application: with 15 competitors, two quitting this year and one changing business models, knowing you're going to keep going while the remaining ones "die" is the mindset that wins long games.

### Quitting is Not an Option

It is not an option to quit. It's just not an option. Remove it from your vocabulary. You cannot leave this content journey.

The pushback on "you are the sum of five people around you": if you live somewhere with basically nobody reasonably successful or on the entrepreneurship journey around you, you can simply decide that quitting is not an option - and become immune to your environment.

## Data Collection During Volume

### What to Track During Volume Phases

During the stretch phase (2-3x/day), track everything:

In this journey you should be collecting so much data. What videos work, what videos don't work, what on-screen text works, what formats work for you.

### 5 Questions for Data Analysis

When analyzing outlier videos (ones that overperformed or underperformed):

1. **What was the hook?** - Was there a number? A keyword? Parentheses? How many words? (Sweet spot is ~6 words)
2. **What was the pacing?** - Break it into sections. How long was each? What was the main theme of each section?
3. **What was the topic/idea?** - What layer of niche vocabulary? Top of the iceberg or deep?
4. **What chapter types were used?** - Story? Framework? Controversial take? Statistic? Famous person reference?
5. **What did comments say?** - Where did people engage? What topics in the comments could become their own video?

### The Feedback Loop (Not Consistency) is What Matters

Reframing the entire "post consistently" advice:

Posting content consistently doesn't matter. In the way that you think.

What consistency actually gives you:

1. More iterations to improve (post, analyze, innovate, repeat)
2. Maintaining your "slot" in followers' algorithmic feeds
3. Faster feedback loops

What consistency does NOT do:

- Train the algorithm to reward you
- Create viewers who "wait" for your content like a TV show schedule

This isn't a Disney channel where Hannah Montana is dropping at eight to seven central. Nobody's waiting for your content.

The baseball analogy: a team gathers data over 7 innings about a pitcher's tendencies. By the 7th inning they know his patterns. Content works the same: you post to gather data, not to train an algorithm.

## Dealing with Burnout and Bad Phases

### Perfectionism is Only a Curse

The two most successful clients (100K+ followers/month average) shared one attribute: they were NOT perfectionists.

Perfectionism is only a curse. It's not a blessing and a curse. It is only a curse. The speed to feedback is what makes a great content creator.

Three manifestations in successful clients:

1. Not afraid to test things without proof of concept
2. Ship content even when they feel terrible on camera
3. Flexible about volume vs quality phases

Ship it anyway. Get better over time. Iterate after you have posted. You're going to put out a C-plus piece of content. That's fine. Because next week it's going to be a B-plus.

### Remixing as a Sustainability Tool

When energy is low, you don't have to create from scratch. Remix your best-performing posts:

- Change the on-screen text (different colors, sizes, placements)
- Add engagement elements (stickers over like button, airplane over send)
- Find your top "followers per view" posts (not just most viral) and remix those specifically

Go find your most followers per view posts. Remix those posts once a week. If you have eight, you should be remixing a post every week - that gives you two months of remixing content.

### The 12% Difference

The difference between being great and being good is only about 12% - but it requires massive effort for small incremental gains:

Five mentors at 1% each = 5%. Three learning trips at 1% each = 3%. Twenty books at 1% per five books = 4%. Total: 12%.

The difference between Tom Brady, the most winning football player of all time, and a guy named Jeff Garcia that you have never heard about, is a 12% difference. Stack these up over time and you will have a crazy nice car, an amazing family, an amazing business.

The insight: it takes 130% effort to get 1% improvement. But done long enough, it will destroy the game.
