# Content as a Science

Content is a science. Most creators treat it as an art form or a personality showcase. That is the wrong frame.

## Core Principles

### Content Is Not a Game of Taste - It's a Game of Knowing

A direct, counterintuitive claim: the three most important traits of a great creator have nothing to do with personality. They are not storytelling, hooks, editing, charisma, or any of the things people assume. Instead, they are data analytics, copywriting, and understanding literary devices.

One principle that captures this entire framework: "Content is not a game of taste, it is a game of knowing. Figure out what, just find ways to know."

The three traits broken down:

**1. Data Analytics**

Given only the data from 10 videos - without seeing the videos themselves, just views, retention, comments, and the theme or three stages of the video - it is possible to create a video that outperforms all 10. The content game is iterative: post, figure out what is winning, take the winners, test more, figure out what is winning, test more. You continue to climb the ladder of data.

**2. Copywriting**

The skill of persuasive language without it feeling salesy. Copywriting is not just writing sales pages - it is the invisible force that makes hooks tangible and specific.

Bad example: "How to get better mental performance on the mound" (vague, generic).
Good example: "How to not get rattled when you throw three balls in a row and come back and deliver 83% more strikes" (specific pain point, specific outcome, specific number).

The difference: the second hook takes an abstract concept (mental performance) and turns it into a scene the viewer has actually experienced. That is copywriting applied to content.

**3. Literary Devices**

Not just "storytelling" but a deep understanding of literature. Metaphors, illustrations, quotes, statistics, foreshadowing, juxtaposition, curiosity loops. Do you understand literature? Do you speak in illustrations, quotes, and statistics? Can you foreshadow later into the video? Do you understand juxtaposition and creating contrast? Do you open curiosity loops and then close them?

These three traits explain why some creators with average personalities dominate, while charismatic creators with zero analytical skills plateau.

### Math + Reading > Energy + Vibes

This is the core philosophy distilled into four words. Content success comes from studying data (math) and reading deeply, not from having good vibes or high energy. The people who consistently win are treating content like a science experiment - forming hypotheses, testing variables, measuring results, iterating based on evidence.

This directly challenges the popular narrative of "just be yourself," "just have fun," "just bring the energy." Those things matter, but they are table stakes. Math and reading are the multipliers.

### How to Analyze Data with a Deck of Cards

A card-based data analysis framework, described as one of the most important practical methods for content growth:

Take three videos: 6K views, 2K views, 7K views. Break each into "chapters" (ingredients) - these are the sections of your video: hook type, story type, structure, action steps.

**Step 1: Eliminate the flop entirely.** The 2K video did not do well. Remove it from the board completely. Do not try to salvage lessons from failures - focus entirely on winners.

**Step 2: Find commonalities between winners.** Look at the 6K and 7K videos. Both had historical stories? Both had numbers? Both had step-by-step processes? Map the overlapping ingredients.

**Step 3: Go to the comments to find which specific element resonated.** Nobody commented about the statistic. A bunch of people asked about the second action step. Comments tell you which ingredient in the winning recipe was the star.

**Step 4: Isolate the winning ingredient and build the next video around it.** If people loved the action step about soil, make the entire next video about soil - not three action steps where soil is just one.

**Step 5: Choose the hook with the best 3-second retention rate.** Your analytics show which hook type held people longest in the first three seconds. Use that hook style for the next video.

Example walkthrough: A gardener has three videos. The winners both featured historical stories and some form of numbers. Comments revealed people loved the specific action step about soil. So the next video combines: a historical story going straight into a single action step about soil, ending with a mathematical breakdown. Result: a 6K-viewed video becomes a 77K video. That is how you use data.

### Ideation Is the #1 Skill - Spend 40% of Your Time on It

The most important part of a video is not the hook, not the edit, not the storytelling, not the pacing. It is the topic.

People think hooks, scripting, and editing make a video go viral. It is the topic. People want to know topics. The best edit in the world on a vague or irrelevant topic gets ignored.

Recommended time allocation for a one-hour content work session:

- **Ideation: ~40%** - Scraping competitor TikToks, YouTube, Reddit, your own data. Making sure topic selection is strong.
- **Growth tactics: ~20%** - Trial reels, comment farming, automation tools
- **Strategy: ~15%** - Balancing top/middle/bottom of funnel content
- **Hooks: ~10%** - Crafting and refining hooks
- **Scripting/storytelling: ~5%** - Brief outlining
- **Editing/production: ~5%** - Minimal editing
- **Copywriting practice: ~5%** - Hand-writing favorite hooks word for word

The ratio is shocking: 40% ideation vs. 5% editing. Most creators invert this - spending hours editing a video about a topic nobody cares about.

**Proof from client work:** Growing a wrestling coach from 0 to 10K followers in 14 days on Instagram was not because of fancy hooks or editing - it was because the creator grew up in the wrestling community, wrestled at the D1 level, and knew the exact topics wrestlers obsess over. "Three ways to get more takedowns" is a terrible topic. "How to eat every single meal and still have a great weight cut" would explode. That is niche vocabulary at work.

### The Niche Vocabulary Framework

Every niche has an iceberg of vocabulary:

**Top of the iceberg (surface vocabulary):** Seeds, planting, soil, tilling - the basics everyone talks about. Videos about these get drowned in competition.

**Below the iceberg (deep vocabulary):** Hydroponics metrics, specific sun ray measurements at 4 PM, the exact inches down for carrot seeds. This is what elite practitioners actually discuss.

The mental exercise: imagine walking into a circular meeting - like an AA meeting - with 20 experts in your niche. What are they talking about? They are probably not talking about the basics. Those 20 experts have concerns that basic creators cannot even articulate, let alone answer.

Application to content creation: Low-level content creators make videos about "how to go viral" - that is top-of-iceberg vocabulary. But in a room of 20 experienced creators, they would be talking about follower quality problems, building communities, brand pillars, feeling pigeonholed, platform expansion. Viral videos that actually resonate are not about going viral - they are about monetization struggles, follower quality, the gap between views and income.

**How to find deep niche vocabulary:**

1. **Reddit** - Go to your niche's subreddits, find what people are actually discussing
2. **Time in niche (TIN)** - The more time you spend in your niche, the deeper your vocabulary naturally goes
3. **Polls** - Run polls on Instagram/TikTok stories, but ask layered questions
4. **ChatGPT deep research** - Use it to get the engine going, then validate with real data
5. **Comments** - What are people arguing about or asking about in comments on your existing videos?

Once you find a deep vocabulary keyword (like "hydroponics" in gardening), move it directly into your on-screen text hook. That is the bridge from research to views.

### Studying Other Industries for Content Ideas

If you want to be great at content, you need to study businesses and how they make money - because the tactics are very similar to how creators get views. The same way Apple became a billion-dollar company is the same way a creator can become a billion-dollar-viewed creator.

The methodology: take principles from other industries - especially historical business stories - and apply them to content creation. History's greatest entrepreneurs offer principles that translate directly.

Specific cross-industry applications:

- **Startup methodology applied to content:** MVC (Minimal Viable Content) to Content Market Fit, borrowed from the startup concept of MVP to Product Market Fit. Y Combinator says nothing else in the world matters except for finding product market fit.

- **A 1977 Wisconsin paper company** discovered that same-day delivery to Chicago - not the salesperson - was what drove revenue. They expanded next-day delivery everywhere and revenue exploded. Applied to content: when a video pops, figure out the real reason (the anomaly) and exploit it everywhere.

- **Liquid Death's counter-positioning** - Went from 0% to 3% of the water bottle market in seven years by being the opposite of its category. Applied to creator strategy: do not copy the market leaders, interrupt the pattern.

- **Amazon's expansion strategy** - Started with books, mastered it, expanded to CDs and movies, then went wide. Applied to niche expansion: crack into the market hyper-niched, expand to adjacent topics, then go wide only after you have conquered your space.

- **FDR's fireside chats** - 31 radio broadcasts studied for pain point awareness and mass communication principles. Applied to understanding your audience's emotional state.

- **Roger Federer's autobiography** - "Mastering the Long Game of Tennis." Applied to longevity, the self-discipline of balance, and thinking in decades not days.

- **Mailchimp's lean strategy** - Stayed profitable from the start with less than 50 employees while 12 VC-funded competitors burned cash. Applied to keeping content team costs low and outlasting bigger operations.

- **Cashvertising's 8 psychological triggers** - Applied to content hooks and topic selection.

If you are a gardener, go study how Elon Musk built Tesla and apply it to your garden. The principles transfer.

### The Barbell Method for Knowledge

A reading system that creates asymmetric advantage:

Read timeless books from as far back as possible - specifically biographies - OR books within the past one to three years that are hyper-practical about today's current landscape. The five-to-ten-year range is neither timeless nor current, and thus not as valuable.

The barbell: old biographies and timeless principles on one end, hyper-recent podcasts and articles on the other. Nothing in the mushy middle.

Why old biographies: they contain timeless principles that never change. Why recent content: it captures today's landscape. Why not the 5-10 year range: it is neither timeless nor current. A 2018 social media strategy book is outdated in specific tactics but not yet timeless in principles.

Example barbell: Read the Steve Jobs biography (old end) and listen to a podcast that came out last week about current algorithm changes (new end). Skip the 2018 social media strategy book.

### Kill the Vibes Around Reading

"Vibes" are the number one killer of reading. Perfectionism traps to eliminate:

- "I can't start a book and not finish it" - needs to die. Give a book 3 minutes. If it is not valuable, move on.
- Buying books for the shelf aesthetic - buying for the look and never reading them
- Having to have a perfect annotation system - color-coded highlights, perfect notes
- Needing 30 minutes of quiet reading time - you do not have that, and you do not need it

The whole point of reading is to change behavior for the better. What changes behavior? Repetition.

It would be better to read one to three books a year and keep reading them over and over for three years than to read 40 books you are never going to remember.

The goal is eventually settling on about 10 books to read every single year, forever. Deep Work by Cal Newport is one that belongs on that list.

### The Four Layers of Memory Applied to Reading

A practical reading system:

1. **Hear it** (Layer 1) - Streaming audio. Listen while driving, walking, watching TV with audio down. Always consuming.
2. **Say it** (Layer 2) - Tell someone what you learned. Speaking forces encoding.
3. **Write it** (Layer 3) - Circle, underline, block text in the physical copy. Voice note into Notes app while driving. At the end of the book, write all takeaways in the front pages.
4. **Teach it** (Layer 4) - Make content about what you learned. This is the deepest memory encoding and also drives your content pipeline.

**The practical workflow:**

- Use streaming audio for unlimited audiobooks (consumption layer)
- Order the physical copy for engagement (retention layer)
- Voice note everything into Notes app while listening
- At the end: write all voice note takeaways in the front pages of the physical book
- Dog-ear memorable stories for quick reference later
- Revisit by flipping through dog-eared pages

### Content Market Fit - The Sprint

Borrowed directly from startup methodology:

**Phase 1: MVC (Minimal Viable Content)** - The easiest possible way to make content. Turn on camera, talk. No editing, no fancy setups. Just the lowest-friction path to posting.

**Phase 2: Volume Sprint** - 100 posts. However you want to get there - three times a day for a month, once a day for three months. The goal is data collection, not perfection.

**Phase 3: Find Content Market Fit** - A theme that consistently hits 5K-15K views within your brand pillars. You will sense it. This is the content equivalent of product-market fit - when you have found the intersection of what you can make, what you enjoy making, and what the market actually wants.

**Phase 4: Accordion method** - Compress effort from 15 posts/week into 5 posts that match your content market fit. Focus only on winners. Quality over quantity now, but informed by data from the volume sprint.

### Exploiting Anomalies - The Three-Layer Analysis

When a video pops, analyze three things:

**Layer 1 - The Hook:**

- Were there numbers in it?
- Were there specific keywords?
- How many words was the hook? (Sweet spot: around 6 for on-screen text)
- Were there parentheses? (Pattern interrupt in text)
- What was the emotional angle? ("I think I just got rich" = vulnerability + surprise)

**Layer 2 - The Pacing:**
Break the video into timed sections and identify the theme of each:

- Was it a historical story?
- Was it action steps?
- Was it a famous person reference?
- Was it a personal story?
- Was it a joke or quote?

Then test: if the video had a section where you talked about someone famous - did that drive retention? If the video had a step-by-step framework - did that hold people through to the end?

**Layer 3 - The Ideation (most important):**
Follow layers of sub-topics deeper and deeper. Own example:

- Videos about carrots did well (first layer)
- Within carrots, "trimming the leaves" sub-topic popped (second layer)
- "Trimming the leaves" also worked when applied to cabbage (cross-testing)
- Within trimming, content about specific utensils/scissors resonated (third layer)
- Now make a "top 10 favorite gardening utensils" video (capitalizing on third layer)

Creators do not follow layers of ideas like this. They do not follow layers.

### The Eight Psychological Triggers from Cashvertising

These eight triggers function as content topic generators:

1. **Enjoyment of life / life extension** - "Eat these three carrots to have more healthy joints in your thirties"
2. **Enjoyment of food or beverages** - "Understand this recipe to have the best pizza you've ever had"
3. **Freedom from fear, pain, or danger** - "Carry this vegetable with you to avoid getting robbed at night"
4. **Sexual companionship** - "Eat this fruit and your downstairs juices will taste better"
5. **Comfortable living conditions** - "This fruit helps you profit more income to get out of debt faster"
6. **Superiority / keeping up with the Joneses** - "Make this recipe when your friends come over to signal high status"
7. **Care and protection of loved ones** - "Make this natural smoothie to help your old mom avoid cancer"
8. **Social approval** - "Make this recipe at the friend dinner so everyone sees you as the backbone of the friend group"

Weaving these into content topics and hooks produces a measurable improvement in performance.

### 99% of Content Tips Are Bullshit

Most content advice circulating on social platforms is noise:

- How to edit
- Which hooks to use
- Which hashtags
- Which time to post

99% of it does not matter. Promise.

What actually matters, rooted in psychology and data:

1. **Transcript editing** - Most people have 20% fluff in their communication. In CapCut desktop, click transcript, drag and cut anything where you repeat yourself or rant about something irrelevant to the main point. That is roughly 20% more views.

2. **Use the Edits app for Instagram** - Instagram is pushing it with more reach because it is their startup. Post with on-screen text, trim, 1.1x speed, post from Edits.

3. **Cross-posting and volume** - A 1:0.8 ratio of posts to dollars. If you post 5 times and make $5, posting 10 times makes $9. That is a strong return. Drop your script into ChatGPT, turn it into a LinkedIn or X post. Hire help to build the SOPs.

4. **Stories are algorithmic** - Drive engagement with easy call-to-action content. Post an early morning story asking people to send a specific emoji. Everyone who sends the emoji back tells the algorithm the story is highly engaged. Later story promotions reach far more people.

5. **Start a second TikTok for research** - Only consume your niche. Look for format opportunities, niche vocabulary, and comment insights.

### Shikaki - The Japanese Art of Changing Behavior Through Design

The concept: design that subconsciously prompts behavior. A basketball hoop over a trash can gets kids to throw trash away. Footprints on a grocery floor direct traffic flow. A speed radar showing your speed makes you slow down.

Applied to content:

- Put "hold for 2x speed" on screen - does that make people watch longer?
- Drop a blue heart emoji next to the like button - does that prompt likes?
- Put a timer across the bottom of the screen - does that increase watch time?
- Place a bookmark icon over the save button - does that prompt saves?

The question many creators miss: so many people care about fancy editing and zooms. Can you change someone's behavior through design?

### Funnel Strategy for Content

The ebb and flow between virality and conversions:

**Crucial distinction:** Having followers does not mean making money. If you are only making money through brand deals and ad revenue, you will hit a ceiling.

**Top of Funnel (reach)** - "Can anyone watch this?" Must be 95% universal appeal. Gardener example: "How to feed a family of five on $10." Almost anyone would be mildly interested.

**Middle of Funnel (nurture)** - "If they applied this, would their life be better?" More niche-specific, deeply valuable. Gardener example: "How to build a hydroponic garden in only 10 square feet."

**Bottom of Funnel (conversion)** - "Did I position my product as the solution while still adding value?" Do not just pitch - include genuine tips that make the viewer's life better even if they do not buy. Gardener example: include tilling tips, watering tips, fertilizer tips alongside your seed product.

**Posting ratio:** About 4:2:1 (top:middle:bottom) for smaller creators. For bigger followings, flip to 2:4:1. Adjust based on whether you want more views (more top) or more money (more bottom). Middle of funnel is always the baseline.

### The Volume-to-Data Feedback Loop

Volume is NOT for reach. It is for data. Three posts per day creates anomalies:

- Day 1: 100 views, 100 views, 400 views. The 400 is an anomaly. Why?
- Day 2: Three videos trying to replicate the 400. Results: 400, 400, 800. The 800 is the new anomaly.
- Day 3: "What was in the 800 video? I was talking about carrots." Three carrot videos: 800, 800, 1500.
- Day 4: "What was in the 1500? Specifically the weight of carrots." Test weight as a concept with other vegetables...

This is how you climb the ladder. Not spraying and praying - running scientific experiments with rapid feedback loops. Each day's data informs the next day's hypothesis.

### Repurposing to Reddit for AI Search

A forward-looking tactic: drop your video script into ChatGPT and say "turn this into an SEO Reddit post." Post it to Reddit. Why? Because AI search tools like ChatGPT and Claude pull results primarily from Reddit and YouTube. As people use AI search instead of Google, getting your content into Reddit positions you for the next wave of discovery.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Spending too much time on editing** - Should be approximately 5% of your time, not 50%
2. **Making videos about surface-level niche vocabulary** - Seeds and soil when your audience wants to talk about hydroponics metrics
3. **Not following layers of ideas** - When a sub-topic pops, most creators do not drill deeper into it
4. **Reading for volume instead of retention** - 40 books you cannot remember vs. 3 books read repeatedly
5. **Reading books from the 5-10 year old middle range** - Neither timeless nor current
6. **Believing "vibes" content advice** - Hashtags, posting times, specific editing tricks
7. **Not cross-posting** - Missing the 1:0.8 ratio of posts to dollars
8. **Making top-of-funnel content and expecting it to monetize** - Views and money come from different types of content
9. **Not using comments as data** - Comments tell you which specific element resonated
10. **Studying only your own niche** - The best insights come from studying businesses and other industries

- Content is not a game of taste, it is a game of knowing.

- Spend 40% of content time on ideation. Most creators invert this ratio.

- Read 1-3 books repeatedly over years rather than 40 books you will never remember.
