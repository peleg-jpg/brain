# Curation and Remixing

Curate in public while you build in private until people beg for what you're building. One more time: curate in public while you build in private until people beg for you to build in public.

## Core Principles

### Curate in Public, Build in Private

This is one of the most-repeated and highest-performing principles in content strategy. It directly challenges the common advice to "build in public" and replaces it with a three-phase framework for new creators.

**The problem with building in public:** Nobody cares about your journey if you haven't done anything yet. Ask yourself: do you care that a 24-year-old kid from Indiana is starting a clothing company and it's day 14? You don't know this kid, they have a hundred followers. No, you don't care. You truly don't care.

Now: do you care if that same person is reflecting on building a one million dollar clothing company? Yeah, okay, yeah.

The difference is results. Nobody follows a journey for the journey's sake. They follow because there's an outcome worth studying. One million dollars isn't even massive, but it's enough to create intrigue. Day 14 of a hundred-follower account creates zero intrigue.

**The three-phase framework:**

**Phase 1: Curate in public** - Share what you are LEARNING, not what you are doing. The distinction is critical. Don't document your day. Document your learnings from the day.

The key insight: you learn through experience, not the story of your experience.

A franchise client example makes this concrete: Instead of "Day 21 of launching my hydration therapy spa franchise" (which nobody cares about), say: "On my 21st day of trying to launch a hydration therapy spa, I almost got screwed over for $36,000 if it wasn't for my lawyer. Here are three tips I learned about negotiating commercial leases."

Both videos document the same journey. The first positions you as a random person doing something. The second positions you as a thought leader sharing hard-won knowledge.

Another example: you need to learn how to saddle a horse because you're training to be a jockey. You spend five hours listening to podcasts about it. Don't make a video about "my journey to becoming a jockey, day 14." Make a video: "I just listened to five hours of podcasts on how to saddle a horse. Here are the three tips I discovered."

**Phase 2: Build in private** - Build your business, skills, and results behind the scenes. Don't document day 14 of your clothing brand. Don't do "my journey to losing 50 pounds." Build the results first.

The example from practice: a creator's very first video was about making $40K/month. They didn't document the 22-month journey of failure, video game addiction, failed launches, and scrambling for $1K retainers. They built in private first, then entered public with a result that demanded attention.

**Phase 3: Build in public once people beg for it** - "Once you have a reason to build in public, once people start asking you to." The trigger is social proof. Have you hit a certain number? Accomplished a certain task? Won a certain event? Do people care about you enough to want the behind-the-scenes?

Building in public is trash until you have a reason.

Important scope: this advice is specifically for beginning creators going from 0 to 20,000 followers. If you already have thousands of followers and social proof, you can start weaving in more building-in-public content. But it should never be the backbone of your content in the early stages.

### Three Ways to Build Authority Without Credentials

Many creators feel they lack the social proof or credibility to make their type of content. A person who wants to teach about brand deals but hasn't had many. Someone who wants to give holistic medical advice but doesn't have a certificate yet. Three paths to credibility when you haven't "earned it" in the traditional sense:

**1. Reference a realistic study** - Let the study be your credibility. "This 2024 study just came out saying that carrots grow 35% better whenever they are under drought." You didn't run the study, but citing it gives your advice a foundation.

Full example: "If your carrots are coming out small, you're probably experiencing this one problem." Then the superhook: "This 2024 study said carrots grow 35% better under drought conditions. What does that tell us? They need consistent moisture. When they're in a drought, they're one third better. Here's what you need to do - put cardboard over the soil to keep the moisture in and simulate the drought environment."

**2. Reference someone famous in the niche** - Their name is your credibility. "I was just listening to a podcast by Kevin Esperito with Epic Gardening and he talked about putting cardboard over your soil."

Critical rule: provide your own take. Don't just repeat what the expert said. Add your perspective, your experience, your application of their insight. Give credit explicitly, then add your own layer. Don't pretend like it's your own genius idea - that's being a scumbag. But referencing it honestly and building on it is legitimate and effective.

**3. Name a universal emotion** - If you can articulate a pain your audience has been feeling but nobody has named, they'll trust you instantly. This is the hardest but most powerful approach.

Example: "A lot of content creators get a big following but have poor follower quality because they built it with entertainment and jokes. Now they're struggling to monetize. They haven't had anybody tell them that." When you say this, creators who've been silently struggling with exactly this problem think: "Oh my gosh, that's exactly what I've been experiencing." That recognition creates trust.

Honesty about the limits: some creators have pushed the envelope, over-exaggerated a little because of insecurity and wanting to earn the click. It's just not worth it. Why build a brand on that?

### The Superhook Framework

Your hook grabs attention (first 3 seconds). Your superhook answers why someone should listen to you (next 5-10 seconds). Three types mapped to the three authority-building methods:

**1. Realistic study superhook** - "This 2024 study said carrots grow 35% better under drought conditions. What does that tell us?" The study number and specificity create instant credibility.

**2. Famous reference superhook** - "I was listening to Kevin Esperito on Epic Gardening and he talked about..." The name of the expert transfers their authority to you.

**3. Universal emotion superhook** - "I just planted 60 carrots and they all came out way smaller than I was expecting. So I had to do some research." Shared experience creates instant trust.

The superhook doesn't have to be about your own achievements. It just has to give the viewer a reason to keep watching. "What I learned from reading 100 books" or "60 minutes from a Diary of a CEO podcast boiled into 60 seconds" - these reference external credibility rather than personal accomplishments.

### Content Remixing Strategy

A sophisticated remixing framework uses the Japanese concept of Shikake - design that shapes behavior:

**Step 1: Find your best posts by followers-per-view** (not most views - most conversion). Go through your analytics and calculate which videos brought the most followers relative to views. A 70K view video that brought double the followers of a 200K view video is your better remixing candidate.

**Step 2: Remix one per week.** If you have 8 top-converting posts, that gives you 8 weeks of remixed content from your best performers.

**Step 3: Beat the visual duplicate detector.** Meta added a system that compares your video visually to previous posts. If it looks too similar, the video gets suppressed to zero views. To beat it, you must change the on-screen text significantly:

- Different colors
- Different sizes
- Different placements on screen
- Different fonts
- Different wordings
- Move text from top to bottom
- Switch from electric blue to red

Make it significantly different from the original posting.

**Step 4: Add a Shikake.** This is the psychological layer that separates basic remixing from genius remixing:

- Place a **blue heart emoji** directly over the like button. It tells people subconsciously: wait, I haven't liked this video yet. It's not red. That prompt drives action.
- Place an **airplane emoji** over the share button to prompt sends
- Place a **bookmark emoji** over the save button to prompt saves

These are design elements that subtly change viewer behavior without asking them to do anything. Like grocery store footprints on the floor, or a basketball hoop over the trash can. Applied to social media, these nudges compound across thousands of views.

### Trial Reels Strategy

Trial reels are among the greatest functions ever given to creators. The strategy:

1. Take one video and create 10 versions with different on-screen text hooks
2. Each version must look visually different enough to avoid the duplicate detector
3. The ONLY metric that matters for trial reels is **skip rate** (also called hook rate) - how many people watched past the first three seconds
4. Not likes. Not comments. Not sends. Not watch time. Nothing else matters in your trial reels besides your skip rate.
5. The version with the lowest skip rate gets moved to your main page or remade with that hook

Out of the 10 videos, you are looking for the lowest skip rate. Everything else is noise until you've found your hook.

Current note: posting from the Edits app straight to your profile is currently performing better than posting trial reels and moving winners over, based on ongoing platform observation.

### Please Stop Copying Big Creators

Copying is dead because market cycles in content move too fast and people get burnt out on seeing the same approach. The real skill is pattern interruption - becoming the Liquid Death of your niche.

**The Liquid Death case study:** In 2018, the water bottle market was dominated by Aquafina (13%), Dasani (9%), Smart Water (7%), Fiji (2%), Evian (2%). Liquid Death launched knowing they had to counter-position to break in. Seven years later, Liquid Death captured 3% market share - ranking them #4 in the entire industry. In creator terms, 3% market share translates to roughly a billion views per year.

The number one skill, not just in content, but in marketing in general, is pattern interruption.

**Three ways to pattern interrupt instead of copying:**

**1. Change your format** - If your niche is full of green screen, go do a vlog. If everyone yaps in the car, do an object lesson. Object lessons in the fitness niche are a perfect example - everyone in fitness does gym selfies and b-roll. Object lessons stand out completely.

**2. Find a better niche pocket** - Three directions:

- **Go up (luxury)** - Horse riding for rich people
- **Go down (beginners)** - Horse riding for college students
- **Go cross (add a flavor)** - Add a dimension that creates overlap. Andrew Huberman added science to self-improvement. Adding a strong, distinct identity to a sales niche is a cross-niche counter-position.

**3. Counter-position your branding and messaging** - This is what Liquid Death did at the brand level. In a niche full of Miami gurus and expensive cars, operating from a modest home in a midwestern city is a counter-position. Whether or not it's actually more trustworthy, it reads that way.

Other counter-positioning angles:

- **Add something to your niche** - Horse riding + AI. Stand out in both worlds.
- **Associate with unexpected references** - Self-improvement associated with science. Athletics associated with killers like Kobe and Jordan, not softer brands. Who do you quote? How do you communicate? Do you use math? Do you reference Steve Jobs?
- **Counter-position with demographics** - Gender, age, race. A woman in high-ticket sales is inherently counter-positioned.
- **Counter-position your packaging** - Blue M&M's in a candy aisle full of warm colors. Whatever pops visually first gets the attention.

### Creativity Is Bridging the World to Your Niche

The framework for generating truly unique content ideas: take something you observed in real life or another niche and bridge it to your world. The only rules are: make it logically connect, and be wild.

You make this journey between something you observed in real life and what you can actually talk about. That's creativity. And in that world, the only rule is to make it logically connect and be wild.

**Detailed example:** Someone sees a video of a guy "talking trash to ChatGPT, drafting WNBA players." Has nothing to do with brand positioning. But bridge it: "What does it look like for you to draft against ChatGPT on the top five clothing companies with the best brand positioning?"

Then layer it deeper:

- First level: generic draft of top 5 companies
- Second level: draft by specific themes - logo only, color only, typography only, in-store design only
- Each layer creates a new video concept from the same creative bridge

The key mental muscle: seeing something in a completely unrelated domain and asking "what's my niche version of this?" Then letting your mind go ten times crazier. The more unrelated the source, the more creative the bridge, the more unique your content.

Another example from practice: seeing a Toyota video criticizing the brand, consuming it as entertainment, then thinking "I could make a video like this about something in my world." That's the bridge - consuming widely and connecting narrowly.

### Standing on the Shoulders of Giants

A significant competitive edge in content comes from studying historical entrepreneurs and applying their principles to content. The Founders Podcast by David Senra is a model for this: you study history's greatest entrepreneurs and take their principles into your domain.

The specific cross-industry applications that have been documented:

| Source              | Original Context                                   | Content Application                             |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Startup methodology | MVP to Product Market Fit                          | MVC to Content Market Fit                       |
| Liquid Death        | Counter-positioned the water market                | Counter-position your niche format              |
| Amazon              | Books to everything                                | Hyper-niche to broad expansion                  |
| 1977 paper company  | Same-day delivery anomaly                          | Exploit viral video anomalies                   |
| FDR fireside chats  | Pain point awareness for mass media                | Describe audience in 2-3 emotions               |
| Roger Federer       | Self-discipline of balance                         | Healthy relationship with content               |
| Cashvertising       | 8 psychological triggers                           | Content hook generators                         |
| Mailchimp           | Low cost, high margins, outlast funded competitors | Keep media team lean, outlast bigger operations |

If you are a gardener, go study how Elon Musk built Tesla and apply it to your garden.

The principle isn't just about borrowing tactics - it's about developing a fundamentally different perspective than other creators in your niche. While they study each other's videos, you're studying Steve Jobs' biography. That asymmetric knowledge creates content that feels fresh because it comes from an entirely different source.

### The 70/20/10 Content Mix

From Caleb Ralston, Alex Hormozi's creative director:

- **70%** - Things already winning. Your proven formulas. Data-backed winners. Guaranteed winners pulled from data.
- **20%** - Tests of improvement. New variations on what works. Incremental innovation.
- **10%** - Completely off the wall. Things that make zero sense. This is where you feel uncomfortable posting. This is where breakthroughs happen.

A practical variation uses 75/25 (proven vs. testing), which combines the 20% and 10% into one bucket. But the critical insight is the same: you MUST allocate time for experiments.

The proof: a 10-minute yapping format - which became a signature style and got 300K views - came from the 10% wild card slot. The creator was trying to make a 5-minute video, yapped too long, almost didn't post it, and it became their most recognized format. Without the testing allocation, they would have stuck with what was "working" and never found their true format.

The agency application: when trying to dethrone a major creator in a niche, the strategy was: "70% will be guaranteed winners that we've pulled from data. 20% we're going to try to improve. 10% we're going to test some wild stuff. No repurposing - everything native. That's how we climb the ladder."

### Using a Second TikTok for Research

Create a new TikTok account. Only consume your niche. Train the algorithm by only engaging with content in your space. Then look for three things:

**1. Format opportunities** - What formats exist in OTHER niches that nobody in YOUR niche is doing? If you're in the gardening niche, there's already a bunch of green screen and vlog gardening. But if you scroll your personal feed and see a DJ do a whiteboard - that makes no sense in context, but think: can you bring the whiteboard format into gardening?

The format bridge: take a format that works in an unrelated niche and transplant it into yours. This is pattern interruption through format innovation, not content copying.

**2. Niche vocabulary and new information** - Was there a video with unique vocabulary that popped? Look for the pattern interrupt in the scroll:

"Three tips for bigger carrots. Five tips for better soil. 10 tips for sunlight. The historical story of Edwin Gutierrez and how he invented the leaf trimmer. Nine tips on how to start an orchard." The pattern interrupt is in the middle. One of those is different. That unique, deeper-vocabulary video is a signal - bring that type of content into your own feed.

**3. Comment insights** - What are people engaging with, arguing about, or asking about in comments? Those arguments and questions are your next video topics. Comments reveal the real pain points and fascinations of your audience.

### Customizing Your Algorithm for Research

A specific, actionable tactic for Instagram:

1. Go to the Reels tab at the bottom of screen
2. Click the two-lines-and-heart icon in the top right
3. You'll see what Instagram thinks you're interested in based on your engagement
4. **Add topics** you want to learn about (AI, content creation, business)
5. **Remove topics** that trigger dopamine traps

The categories to see less of: things that trigger old habits, dopamine cravings, or other time-wasters. Actively pruning these reclaims massive amounts of research time.

The math: the average person spends five hours on social media a day. If you take 40% of that for 365 days and make it more curated, you gain over 700 hours of an advantage compared to the people around you.

700 hours per year. That's the difference between consuming your algorithm passively and curating it intentionally.

### How to Adapt Content Without Being a Copycat

The distinction between copying and adapting is central to this framework. Copying = taking someone's exact format, topic, and hook and reproducing it in your niche. Adapting = taking a principle from outside your niche and expressing it through your unique lens.

**The right way to reference others:**

- Give credit explicitly - "I was listening to Alex Hormozi on a podcast and he said..."
- Add your own take on top of their insight
- Use studies, famous names, and universal emotions as credibility bridges
- Reference somebody famous as a "superhook" - the second or third sentence

**What NOT to do:**

- Pretend it's your genius idea
- Lie about your credentials or experience
- Just repeat what someone else said without adding perspective
- Copy formats 1:1 from big creators in your own niche
- Rip off a viral video and hope people follow you instead of the original

You can pull from somebody else. Use it wisely. Give them credit. Don't pretend like it's your own genius idea. Or else you're being a scumbag.

The reason copying fails strategically: people will always follow the person who made the first piece of content. Even if your copy gets views, the follows go to the original creator. You get views, they get followers. That's a losing trade.

### The Algorithmic Support System

A community-based growth tactic:

Three things to do for your creator friends (and they do for you):

1. **Create a saved folder called "algo"** - Save your friends' and favorite creators' videos into it. You never have to look at it again. The save alone boosts their video.

2. **Find one friend to send videos to** - Just DM them the video and type "algo." That's it. One share and one save can do a lot for a video. The impact of a single share and save is underestimated by most creators.

3. **Argue in the comments** - Drive engagement through genuine discussion and friendly debate in each other's comments. Someone who argues with people and keeps the conversation going in your comments is genuinely valuable. People argue back, and the comment count runs up.

### Content Scaling Through Clipping Ecosystems

For creators who've already conquered their niche (100K-300K+ followers), a scaling framework inspired by the highest-growth creators:

**Raw footage pipeline:** Accumulate 2-3 hours of raw footage per day from:

- Podcasts (~25% of total footage)
- Lifestyle content / vlogs
- Business meeting recordings
- Videographer following you for a few hours

**Clipping with re-hooking:** Don't just clip individual segments. Each clip should be turned into 10 videos with 10 different hooks. 10 hours of content becomes approximately 4,000 pieces of content.

**Distribution strategy:**

- Secondary TikTok accounts for testing
- Winners move to Instagram trial reels
- Data from 4,000 posts informs what goes on your main profile
- You might do 4,000 pieces of content and out of all that, get three pieces that are clear winners. Those go on the main page.

**Team structure:** 4-5 editors from the Philippines, briefed with a playbook. Cost: approximately $10,000 for 4,000 pieces of content. Compare to a 18-20 person media team at $1M+/year.

**Funnel layering:** Each editor has a different funnel assignment:

- Top-of-funnel clipper sends traffic to YouTube and Instagram
- Middle-of-funnel clipper sends traffic to email sequences
- Bottom-of-funnel clipper sends traffic to landing pages and calls

### The Five Categories of Uniqueness for Follower Growth

Not all views convert to followers equally. Five categories, all requiring uniqueness:

1. **Unique value** - Are you giving advice they haven't heard before? If you're repeating what bigger creators say, they already have that follower slot filled. There's no reason to follow you when the original source is one search away.

2. **Unique perspective** - Can you change their worldview? Not tips but a new way of thinking about something entirely.

3. **Unique humor** - Most people aren't funny. Don't force this if it's not natural. But if you genuinely are, and your humor is unique, it's powerful.

4. **Empathy** - Relatability through shared experience. An amputee making content about having one arm - amputees will follow immediately. The shared identity creates instant loyalty.

5. **Aspirational content** - The hardest to convert from. Most people's lives are not uniquely aspirational. A generic day-in-the-life going to a normal gym, eating normal food, with a mildly decent body converts almost nobody.

Apply uniqueness to at least ONE of these five categories. That's where follower conversion actually happens.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Building in public before you've built anything** - Nobody cares about day 14 of your clothing brand with 100 followers
2. **Sharing the story of your experience instead of your learnings** - "On day 21 I did this" vs. "On day 21 I learned these three things about commercial leases"
3. **Copying big creators' formats 1:1** - Market cycles move too fast, and followers go to the original
4. **Not using a second TikTok for research** - Missing format opportunities, niche vocabulary, and comment insights
5. **Remixing without beating the visual duplicate detector** - Reposting the same video gets zero views
6. **Looking at views instead of followers-per-view for remixing candidates** - The best remixing candidates are high-conversion, not high-view
7. **Pretending other people's insights are your own** - That's being a scumbag
8. **Only studying your own niche** - The best ideas come from cross-industry bridges
9. **Not customizing your algorithm for research** - Letting dopamine traps eat 700 hours/year of potential advantage
10. **Trying to be aspirational without a uniquely aspirational life** - Generic day-in-the-life content doesn't convert
11. **Treating the 70/20/10 split as optional** - Without the 10% wild card, you'll never find breakthrough formats

## Tactical Playbook Summary

### Weekly Curation Routine

1. Scroll your second TikTok (niche-only) for 30 min - note formats, vocabulary, comment patterns
2. Read Reddit threads in your niche - look for deep vocabulary and heated discussions
3. Listen to one podcast outside your niche - look for bridgeable concepts
4. Review your analytics - find top followers-per-view posts for remixing
5. Customize your Instagram algorithm settings monthly

### Remixing Checklist

1. Identify posts with best followers-per-view ratio
2. Change on-screen text: different color, size, placement, font, wording
3. Add a Shikake: heart over like button, airplane over share, bookmark over save
4. Post one remix per week
5. Track skip rate on trial reel versions - only move lowest-skip to main page

### Content Idea Generation

1. Bridge: "What's the [thing I just saw] version for my niche?"
2. Study cross-industry: What did [historical entrepreneur] do that applies to my content?
3. Niche vocabulary: What would 20 experts in my niche be arguing about?
4. Comment mining: What are people asking about in comments on my best posts?
5. 10% wild card: What's the craziest idea I can post this week?

## Key Principles Summary

- Curate in public (share learnings), build in private (build results) - until people beg for behind-the-scenes
- Three paths to authority without credentials: studies, famous names, universal emotions
- The superhook is your "why should you listen to me" - can reference external credibility, not just personal achievement
- Remix by followers-per-view, not total views - conversion is the signal
- Beat the duplicate detector with significantly different on-screen text
- Add Shikake elements to nudge behavior without asking explicitly
- Trial reels: only skip rate matters, nothing else
- The 70/20/10 split is non-negotiable - without 10% wildcards, you won't find breakthroughs
- Copying fails because followers go to the original creator, not the copy
- Creativity = bridging unrelated domains to your niche, the more unrelated the better
- Study history's great entrepreneurs - asymmetric knowledge creates fresh content
- Curate your own algorithm or it will eat you alive - 700 hours/year at stake
- Give credit when referencing others, then add your own perspective on top
