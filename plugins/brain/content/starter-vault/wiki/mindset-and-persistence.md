# Mindset and Persistence

The income shift from $3,000 to $40,000 a month in a 60-day window traced back to one mental model: most people play not to lose. They want to get barely ahead. They are not playing to truly crush.

## Core Principles

### Playing to Win vs. Playing Not to Lose

The mental model here draws from the book Hardball and Kobe Bryant's early NBA philosophy. Bryant entered the league and observed that most players were playing for financial security. He decided to run circles around them instead.

Most people in business and content are trying not to fail. They want small, safe wins - one to two-point victories. They take a client for $1K and feel relief instead of hunger. They get 5K views and think "okay, that is enough." The few who play to win - who try to score 50 points instead of protecting a 2-point lead - have almost no competition because the mindset itself is the barrier.

Play to dominate. Try to score 50, 20, 30, 40 points compared to small one or two-point wins.

The Hardball concept of "exploiting anomalies" applies directly to content: when a paper company in Wisconsin discovered same-day delivery was their edge, they scaled it everywhere. Take the same approach with viral content - find what is winning and ruthlessly double down, do not just be grateful it happened once.

### The Work-to-Reward Ratio

The single biggest difference between the $3K version and the $40K version of yourself often comes down to understanding the actual work-to-reward ratio required for the lifestyle you want.

It is not an ignorance problem. It is not an effort problem. The question is: do you actually understand that what you want in life - pick a number or a lifestyle - requires the specific amount of work you are currently putting in?

The problem is self-deception. People genuinely believe they are working hard enough, but they are not. Playing video games for 2-3 hours a day is fine, right? Sleeping until 8 is fine. Taking some days off is fine. But these things compound into a massive deficit. You do not understand how a few hours a day will change your life over time. You are underestimating what you can do in a year.

**The wrestling story:** A state championship win in wrestling was, in retrospect, the worst thing that could happen - because it was not earned. The top-ranked competitor moved away, there was an injury, and it was luck. Then college wrestling revealed the truth: roommates who wrestled D1 worked approximately 600% harder. And they still were not reaching the goals that felt attainable at a lower work level. That changed everything.

**The sacrifice math:** Stopping a workout routine for nine months to sprint a business is a real trade-off. Working out 3-4 times per week costs roughly 8 hours per week when you factor in travel, the workout, showering, and eating after. Over nine months, that is approximately 120 hours. You have no idea what you can do in 120 hours.

The work-to-reward ratio is not just about working harder - it is about honestly assessing what you are willing to trade and accepting the ceiling that creates.

The challenge: do not try to defend this. Do not try to justify it away. Instead, try to convince yourself that it is true - that you have a bad work-to-reward ratio. That belief will change your life.

### Admit It Is Gonna Be Hard, Then Just Start

The most fundamental belief in this framework: the path is brutal, the timeline is long, and almost nobody will actually do it. But the math of competition is in your favor precisely because of that.

A realistic framework can lead someone to $10K a month. But maybe 0.1% of people who encounter it will actually execute it. That is just the reality.

A real timeline looks like this: two years total - one year learning, one year building. Within the building year, it took from February to December to crack $10K/month. Then compounding took it quickly higher. The timeline is not sugarcoated:

People want to rush the process. The learning took a year. The building took another year.

The honesty is strategic: by admitting the difficulty upfront, you filter out people who would quit and speak directly to the ones who will persevere. When people respond with "I do not believe you" or "you are a scammer" - those are the ones not in the game. Less competition.

### Stay in the Game at All Costs

The single most important skill in content creation is not hooks, storytelling, or editing. When 11 critical creator skills are laid out and an audience is asked to pick the most important one, there is only one correct answer:

Having a healthy relationship with content to post every single day. This is the combination of joy plus obsession. Without this, you will quit. And everything else on the list - great storytelling, great hooks - does not matter if you do not post every single day.

**The Roger Federer framework:** Federer's autobiography, "Mastering the Long Game of Tennis," contains the central lesson in its title. Federer's approach to sustainability becomes a content philosophy:

- When Federer was not feeling it, he did not quit - he went to the gym and just talked to his trainer
- After losses, he would cry, eat with family, then come back
- He practiced "the self-discipline of balance" - even when he wanted to train, sometimes he forced himself to go on vacation
- He never switched sports. He never started over. He stayed in tennis for decades.

Applied to content: play this game for 10 years. Do not change your niche frequently.

Do not think in days. Think in decades. The goal is to be making content for 10 to 20 years, not just a few months.

The niche-switching trap is real: if you are in fitness and you burn out, you can pivot to nutrition (stay in the family tree). But fitness to space engineering means losing your compound. Creators who change niches every few months destroy the compound interest that makes content careers exponential.

### Do Not Be Afraid to Flop

The best part about a flop is that no one saw it.

A testing ratio of 75/25 - 75% proven content, 25% experiments - works well. A commonly used split from the content world (credited to Caleb Ralston, creative director for major content operations):

- **70%** - Things that are already winning. Your proven formulas.
- **20%** - Tests of improvement. New variations on what works.
- **10%** - So absolutely off the wall that they make zero sense.

The 10% wild card slot is where breakthroughs are born. The 10-minute unscripted video format was born from this exact philosophy: TikTok extended its timer to 10 minutes. A video was accidentally recorded way too long. Instead of cutting it down, it went out as the 10% experiment. It got 300,000 views and became a signature format that never would have been found any other way.

You have to be so okay with videos not performing because you will not improve without that tolerance. The true format cannot be found without a 10-25% testing allocation.

### Detachment from Perfectionism

Perfectionism in content is a curse - not a blessing, not a mixed bag. Just a curse.

This is not like the stories about Kobe Bryant or Steve Jobs and their obsessive perfectionism. In content, feedback loops change every 24 hours. You are not making five movies in a lifetime where each one has to be perfect. You are in a system where the speed of feedback is what makes a great creator.

Evidence from agency data: out of all clients tracked, the two who averaged 100,000 followers per month shared one underlying attribute - a complete lack of perfectionism. This showed up in three specific ways:

1. **They were not afraid to test at scale** - They did not need "proof of concept" before posting. They let the team test wildly.
2. **They did not let bad filming days slow production** - If they felt terrible about a recording, they still sent it to the team and let them post it.
3. **They let the team balance volume vs. quality** - When the team said "post more," they posted more. When the team said "dial back and go higher quality," they did that too. Zero ego.

The war cry: go to war with perfectionism inside of you. Be testing 30% of the time with stuff that does not make sense. If you are not feeling great on camera, it does not matter. Ship it anyway.

The compounding effect: you put out a C+ piece of content. That is fine. Next week it will be B+. The week after, A+.

### The 250 People Principle (Risk Is Not Real)

A mathematical breakdown of why competition and risk are illusions. Using a group of 250 people to illustrate what actually happens:

**First 100 people - Never start.** They think it is too risky. They like their job too much. They are worried about depleting savings or what family will think. Some "kind of do it on the side, secretly" but never really go all in.

**Next 100 people - Start but quit.** They hit their first wall around month 2-3 when excitement wears off. They encounter their first real cost, their first rejection, their first doubt. Shiny object syndrome kicks in. They think they are in the fight. They are actually not.

**49 of the remaining 50 - Fail for three specific reasons:**

1. **Not working hard enough** - They genuinely think they are working hard enough but they are not. A true time audit reveals quick grocery runs in the middle of the day, video games late at night, Thursday night out, then sleeping in Friday.

2. **Not investing enough** - In themselves, their business, coaching, tools. They skip dinner with successful people because they do not want to spend the money. Starting with zero dollars and investing in yourself is the move. All you have to do is win the competition at your current level, then move to the next.

3. **Not willing to suffer enough** - Making emotional decisions when things go bad. They flail whenever they have a bad day. A great entrepreneur can move on emotionally unwavering when affliction comes.

**1 person - Truly in the game.**

Risk is not real. It is a math equation and people do not see it that way.

**The gardening math that proves it:** About 50,000 gardener creators exist. If only 1 out of 250 is truly in the game, that is 200 real competitors. The gardening creator economy is approximately $500M. That is $2.5M per serious creator.

The world favors people who create things and punishes consumers.

A referenced framework (from Sean Puri): out of 100 people, the first 80 never start, the next 10 quit too early, 5 chase shiny objects, 2 get unlucky, and 3 are your actual competition.

### The Four Stages of Success

A creator journey ladder from zero to 100K followers:

**Stage 1: Skill Set (0-1K views per video)**

If you do not have the skill set of short form content, nothing else matters. Your niche does not matter. How often you post does not matter. Monetization does not matter. You need to consistently hit 1,000 views 3-4 times per week before advancing.

What to do: post once a day. Invest in learning - courses, books, YouTube videos, podcasts. Analyze creators in your niche. Think about hooks, captions, topic selection, buzzwords. Takes about 100 iterations to develop the skill set.

Real example: posting 20-30 pieces of content and getting only a couple hundred views. After posting 500 pieces across client accounts, the skills were in place - and the first personal video performed well.

**Stage 2: Volume (1K-5K views, ~1.5K followers)**

Now stretch the accordion. Post 2-3 times daily, especially on TikTok. The critical warning that every creator panics about:

When you post three times a day, views get diluted into three videos. If you are usually getting 5,000 views a day, that breaks into 2,000, 2,000, 2,000. But if you look at week-over-week views, your channel is growing and that is what matters.

Run this playbook as long as you can. Collect massive amounts of data: what videos work, what on-screen text works, what formats work for you. Use the 70/30 split - 70% proven winners, 30% wild card experiments.

**Stage 3: Growth Tactics (5K-10K followers)**

Now and only now do the micro-optimizations matter: clothing contrast, captions, trial reels, 1.1x speed, visual pacifiers like holding a pen. Until you have hit exit velocity on your channel, these are noise.

The compounding math: moving videos up 10% on average through growth levers means 10% more followers, maybe 20% more views because of compounding. More followers means more views means more engagement means further reach in the algorithm. Compounding interest.

**Stage 4: Monetization (10K+ followers)**

Three sub-stages of monetization, in strict order:

1. **Service first** - Trade time for money at a premium. One-on-one, high-ticket. Do not think about scale yet. If you think about scaling this early, you will struggle. Example: a fitness creator calling every local gym and offering a $1K rehabilitation course for their members.

2. **Raise prices, then coaching** - Start with one-on-one consulting, then move to group consulting. Scale through pricing so you maintain quality. Cap here before going further.

3. **Digital products last** - Skool communities, courses, etc. Do not think about these until you are at $20, 30, 40, 50K a month.

### This Can Not Be a Hobby - It Has to Be a Calling

The question of finding a niche is reframed as finding a calling. Drawing from Matthew McConaughey's Diary of a CEO appearance:

Three questions to find your niche/calling:

1. **Is it in your DNA?** - Look at your story, your childhood, your natural gifts, the people who impacted you, how you have impacted others. Many people realize in retrospect they always had moments of creative energy but lacked an outlet for it.

2. **Can you outwork and outlast everyone in it?** - You do not know for sure where burnout is, but is this something you can work harder at for longer? If you are seeing your niche as a 3-month or 6-month test before pivoting, this is the wrong mindset.

3. **Can you make a living off it?** - There is a spectrum: services to rich people (top right) to products for poor people (bottom left). If you cannot provide services to rich people, you are not in a financially savvy niche.

You need to reframe finding a niche - it is not about a niche, it is a calling. It is what you want on your gravestone. When you find that answer, you run it into the ground for the next 40, 50, 60 years.

The book Mastery by Robert Greene reinforces this: your niche/calling is found through action, not contemplation.

### You Cannot Post Whatever You Want

A brutally honest take on creator selfishness:

Where else in the world do you get to have success doing whatever you want? If the quarterback of a football team said he was just going to do whatever he wanted today, that would be a problem. If Pizza Hut decided on Tuesdays to become a bookstore, that would be a problem.

The "you are the niche" advice applies to maybe 1% of creators. Most people do not have a 10 out of 10 personality and the gift of the gab.

Instead, follow the Amazon expansion blueprint:

1. **Crack into the market** - Be hyper-niched, specific. Get your first 10K followers.
2. **Expand a little** - Add one or two adjacent layers. Get to 250K followers.
3. **Go wide** - Only after you have conquered your niche, have a team, and are at 500K+ followers can you talk about whatever you want.

## A Real Timeline: $3K to $40K/Month

A full story, not the highlight reel:

**May 2023** - Age 23-24. Quit a $3K/month job as a college assistant pastor. No college degree. Both parents were teachers. Partner making about $6K. Plans to become a creator, podcaster, YouTuber.

**May 2023 - February 2024 (9 months of failure):**

- Playing 3-5 hours of video games daily was very low, supposedly.
- Could never get into deep work. Irresponsible spender.
- But simultaneously believed in the vision and was willing to do more than anyone else.
- Drove 10 hours to Texas 3-4 times to record in-person podcasts. Nobody else was doing that.
- Invested $500 in a one-hour coaching session on social media strategy.
- Tried to be a video game streamer at the same time - 4-6 hours/day gaming.
- Self-aware in retrospect: "I was lying to myself, I just wanted to play video games."
- Posted a podcast every two weeks, ripped short-form content for it.
- Key learning: if you want to be successful in business, the fastest way is to get really good at a skill and sell it to rich people.

**February 2024** - Baby coming in one month. Time to grow up.

- Invested $7K in a writing mentorship program without telling partner.
- In retrospect: making a major financial decision like that without the other person is not being a team unit, especially a month away from becoming parents.
- But because of the $7K investment, there was no choice but to go all in. No more gaming.

**February - June 2024:**

- Trying to be an email marketer, newsletter creator, lead magnet builder.
- Free work for everyone. Building email lists, creating lead magnets.
- In every coaching call, listened to every video, did all homework, asked questions.
- Tons of cold outreach trying to get first client.
- Small pivot: trying to become a "growth operator" - partnering with creators.
- Flew to Jacksonville for a creator with a big following. Recorded two days of content. Built a course. Made $150 in sales. Got a $2.5-3K setup fee. First couple thousand dollars online, about a year into the journey.
- The failed launch hurt. The partner bailed.

**Summer-Fall 2024:**

- Got a part-time job back in ministry ($3K/month) to pay bills.
- Partner's maternity paychecks ran out. Living on $3K/month total.
- Joined another mentorship program ($4-5K more).
- One more pivot into content strategy specifically - a lot of people wanted it.
- Stacked three $1K local retainers through cold and warm outreach.
- Combined with part-time job: $6K/month. First time in two years enough money to pay the bills. Partner's savings had been depleted.

**Late 2024:**

- Got great results for clients - millions of views.
- Spun results into a referral for first major client: $8-9K/month. Life changing.
- Started working for a mentor doing marketing consulting for nine-figure companies.
- Things snowballed.

**Early 2025:**

- Snowball effect: multiple $5-10K retainers stacking up.
- Fired the three $1K clients.
- Got to pick and choose clients.
- Reached $40K/month.

**Total investment in mentorship over two years: $28,000.** The analysis: each mentorship maybe got 1% better. Reading 20 books - another 4%. Flying across the country three times - another 3%. Stacked together, that is the 12% difference between the absolute best and someone who was very good.

### The 130% Effort for 1% Improvement

One thing: it does not take 1% effort to get 1% improvement. It actually takes something like 130% effort to get 1% improvement. But if you do that long enough, you will destroy the game.

## Tactical Playbook

### The Playbook for Making First 10K/Month

Step-by-step framework with specific numbers:

1. **Pick a hard skill** from this list of 10: copywriting, paid ads, landing page design, video editing, funnel building, email marketing/automations, data analysis/consulting, remote sales, content strategy, AI workflows. Pick one and treat it like it is your calling on Earth.

2. **Read 10 books, listen to 100 podcasts.** You can do that in less than a month, but most people will not. This puts you in the top 5-10%. It is compounding - you cannot stop at 5 books and 30 podcasts because the curve goes exponential.

3. **Cold outreach 500 people to do it for free.** Free work is the shortcut. Send 10 DMs/emails a day for 50 days. You are now 2.5 months in and probably have not made a dollar yet.

4. **Do the free work and get great results.** May take 2-3 free clients before you nail it.

5. **Cold outreach another 500 people. Charge $1K per project.** Put together a $1K package that massively overdelivers. De-risk the offer: "If I do not make your money back, you get it for free." At a higher level: "If you do not make your money back, I will actually pay you $1K for wasting your time."

6. **Stack $1K clients until $3-5K/month.** Get out of survival mode. You cannot pitch big clients from a place of desperation.

7. **Go for whale clients ($8-12K/month).** Do not flinch. People will believe you are worth it at $8K. Pitch $10K, take it or leave it.

8. **Let desperation leave.** You have to let your desperation leave, which then ironically makes you more appealing. When you can say no to 12 clients and wait for the right one, your close rate goes way up because you have leverage.

### Morning Mindset Routine

A morning exercise done roughly 90% of mornings:

**Mindset fuel:** Reading that grounds you in faith and purpose, then motivational content in the background. Having Goggins or similar playing while writing emails - it sounds corny, but belief in yourself drains fast and needs to be refilled.

**The Notepad Exercise** (yellow notepad, $6 at Target):

1. **Five things you are grateful for** - Must be specific and not repeated for a long time. Why specific? As you raise levels in business, it is easy to lose your gratefulness for where you have been.

2. **To-do list** - Three to five items max. Write "SPRINT" next to it as a reminder. Sprinting means doing tasks at a speed that demands focus - no phone checking, no running errands, no distraction breaks.

3. **Three things to watch out for** - Can be physical (checking phone too much, saying yes to miscellaneous tasks) or emotional (anger, resentment, lying to yourself). Example: "I have to literally say no to miscellaneous tasks today. People are going to ask me for something and that is going to eat up my day."

4. **Three things to strive for** - Goals beyond the to-do list. Things that would make it a "crushed" day. Example: completing a full workout instead of cutting it short.

This is adapted from an Andrew Huberman exercise, applied to entrepreneurship.

### The Accordion Method for Burnout Prevention

The accordion metaphor (credited to Caleb Ralston, Alex Hormozi's and Gary Vaynerchuk's creative director) describes the rhythm of compressing and expanding effort throughout your creator journey:

**Phase 1 - Compress (learning):** Post once a day. Focus on getting 1% better per post. Learn the skill set.

**Phase 2 - Expand (growth):** Once you can consistently hit 1K views, stretch the accordion. Post 2-3 times daily. Views per video will drop but weekly views skyrocket. Your channel is growing and that is what matters.

**Phase 3 - Compress (plateau/reinvention):** When you hit a plateau - and you will - do not repivot, do not change niches. Compress back to one really good video per day. Find your top-performing data. Start a series. Hire an editor. Go higher production. The way through a plateau is usually through quality.

**Phase 4 - Expand again (scaling):** With new formats and a team, expand back to 2-3 posts daily. This cycle repeats throughout your entire creator career.

Critical warning: everyone who works through this freaks out in the compression-to-expansion moment. Views get diluted into three videos. But the channel is growing. If you are worried about the aesthetic of numbers on your profile, get over it.

Stay in the family tree of your niche when you compress. Fitness to nutrition is fine. Fitness to space engineering means losing your compound.

### The Post-Something-Stupid Challenge

Vulnerability as a growth accelerator. An unscripted talking-head video is the purest form of who you are - no scripting, no editing, just grip it and rip it. The willingness to post something that might flop, something that feels stupid, something where you are not performing - that is what builds the deepest connection with an audience.

The best creators post even when they feel terrible about what they recorded. They do not let a bad filming day slow down production. A C+ piece of content today becomes B+ next week and A+ the week after.

### Survivor Bias and Realistic Creator Journeys

Creator comparison creates a distorted view. Your algorithm only shows you great creators making great content. It gets shown to more people and you are the more people. You never see realistic creator journeys.

A real client journey (documented to show the true process):

- First 30 videos on old account: nothing
- Made a new account. First 45 videos: 100-300 views. Still in low-view territory.
- Week after week of testing - backgrounds, glasses, object lessons, different formats
- 60-70 videos in: conversations about changing formats and going higher total addressable market
- Week 9 (~80 videos in): started getting 1-2K viewed videos by combining personal travel stories with green screen
- Then: first 160K viewed video
- Then: 1.7 million views on a replicatable format

This is the creator journey for 99% of people. Most people do not know how to iterate week over week. They spray and pray. It does not work.

### Five Things to Separate You in Your 20s

A framework for young people building wealth:

1. **Pick a vehicle that actually creates wealth** - Not a hobby like streaming video games
2. **Kill micro-addictions** - Video games, porn, sleeping in
3. **Stack wealth habits** - Do not be afraid to get married and have kids. Read autobiographies instead of fantasy novels.
4. **Take more risk** - Taking 30 times more risk than peers is not recklessness - it is how you advance
5. **Be willing to do a lot for a little amount of progress** - The 130% effort for 1% improvement principle

## Common Mistakes

1. **Thinking you are working hard enough** - Almost nobody has an accurate assessment of their work-to-reward ratio
2. **Waiting for the "right time" to start** - 100 out of 250 people never start at all
3. **Switching niches at the first plateau** - You are destroying compound interest
4. **Perfectionism in any form** - It is only a curse, never a blessing, in content
5. **Playing not to lose** - Small safe wins instead of swinging for 50 points
6. **Thinking free work is beneath you** - Free work is the shortcut
7. **Pitching whale clients from desperation** - Desperation repels; leverage attracts
8. **Trying to be the niche** - Only 1% of people have the personality to talk about whatever they want
9. **Making emotional decisions after a bad day** - Great entrepreneurs move on "emotionally unwavering"
10. **Seeing the timeline in months instead of decades** - Think in decades
11. **Trying to build a Skool community too early** - Wait until $20-50K/month minimum
12. **Building in public before you have built anything** - Nobody cares about day 14 of your clothing brand

## Key Principles (Condensed)

- Most people play not to lose. The ones who dominate play to win. That mindset gap is almost all the competition there is.
- Risk is a math equation. 249 out of 250 people will quit or never start. 1 is your actual competition.
- The work-to-reward ratio is the real problem. Most people are self-deceived about how hard they are actually working.
- The timeline is 2+ years for most people. Nobody who told you otherwise had a real story.
- Stay in the game. Posting every day with a healthy relationship to content beats every other skill.
- Perfectionism is only a curse. Speed of feedback is what makes a great content creator.
- Do not think in days. Think in decades.
